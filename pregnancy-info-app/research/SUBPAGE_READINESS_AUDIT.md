## Content and density standard
Use layered reading, not identical article lengths. Quick orientation and meaningful one-paragraph development milestones may remain short. For consequential preventive choices, medicines, nutrient deficiency, conflicting research, supplement quality and safety, provide additional depth. Describe biological role, food/other source where applicable, relevant outcome, study design/population, comparison, uncertainty, actual material funding/COI, harms/interactions, applicability, and a realistic question for a clinician. Distinguish total nutrient adequacy from added supplement dose; deficiency treatment from prevention; risk-based medication from universal guidance; official safety advice from experimental evidence; human pregnancy outcomes from mechanism or animal studies; and prenatal from lactation/infant evidence. No personal product rankings, dose schedules, self-start/stop medication instructions or automatic reminders. Keep urgent safety visible and accessible. Avoid a long dense opening or padded article. In the 97 focused drafts, 66 have body text under 80 words and 95 under 120 words (excludes title, source list, links and summary/question). This is a prioritized editorial backlog, not a requirement to mechanically lengthen milestones. Most short entries are developmental explanations. Higher-stakes topic synthesis needs further source-level appraisal and deeper evidence before release.

## Evidence priorities for future work
1. Established preventive needs and medication review before conception; folic acid/MTHFR claims and neural tube prevention. Choline, iodine, B12, DHA and iron deserve individual accessible pages. Avoid unsupported cognition enhancement claims. Choline positive trial and follow-up share a cohort; report mixed public/industry-related funding and separate null trial.
2. Risk-based preeclampsia prevention: clinician-assessed aspirin eligibility/timing, calcium evidence and baseline dietary intake. Never conflate preventive aspirin exception with other NSAID use. Vitamin D deficiency versus population prevention; held regimen source remains held.
3. Nausea: B6/doxylamine guidance and ginger evidence, sedating product duplication, severe vomiting escalation. Pain/fever: active ingredients, FDA NSAID warning around 20 weeks, acetaminophen observational designs and confounding, indication and combination-product risks. No prescription substitution.
4. Less-visible nutrients: magnesium, zinc, vitamin A (preformed vs carotenoids), B12 and restricted diets. Food/label/upper-limit interpretation; no unverified dose or brand ranking.
5. Emerging supplements, herbs, creatine, probiotics and collagen: direct human evidence versus preliminary mechanisms; unknown funding remains unknown; no experimental routine, no safety inference from natural origin. Preserve probiotic preeclampsia safety signal and all source holds. 
6. Remaining focused topics to investigate and appraise: medication-specific preconception review (including teratogenic and essential medicines), common thyroid/diabetes/mental-health treatment considerations, prescription versus OTC ingredients, interactions, anemia and gestational diabetes pathways, maternal vaccines by jurisdiction, postpartum/lactation medication compatibility and infant nutrition; separate education and clinician decisions. For each topic use the existing research corpus first, then fill gaps with independent primary studies, official guidance and transparent COI. These are work items, not completed articles or care recommendations. No new chart release or individualized medical plan.
