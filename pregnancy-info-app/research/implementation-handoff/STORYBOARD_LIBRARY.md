# Original short-form storyboard briefs

These are production briefs for Claude, not finished graphics or verified videos. Each has a short narrated script, visible safety note and source IDs in MOBILE_MICROCOPY.json. Keep essential safety available before playback, add captions and accessible alternatives, and obtain clinician review for medical illustrations. No procedure how-to or misleading transformations.

## SKIN-001 — Can creams prevent stretch marks?
- Opening takeaway: Moisturizers can help dryness. Reliable stretch-mark prevention is not established.
- Visual sequence: Show a skin cross-section with a surface moisture layer and deeper striae; avoid showing guaranteed prevention.
- Closing action: Moisturize for comfort, not as a required prevention routine.
- Always visible: During pregnancy, check ingredients, especially retinoids. Stop irritating products. Prevention studies give little information about uncommon adverse effects.
- Sources: S001, S002, S004, S006, S008, S009, S010, S204, S206, S216. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.

## SKIN-002 — Is collagen worth it?
- Opening takeaway: Adult skin studies disagree. Pregnancy stretch-mark prevention is unproven.
- Visual sequence: Show adult skin measurements versus the pregnancy outcome, then the evidence disagreement.
- Closing action: Keep collagen off the required checklist.
- Always visible: Before conception, adult cosmetic findings apply only to similar populations. During pregnancy and lactation, product-specific safety and benefit for this indication remain unestablished.
- Sources: S011, S013, S014, S015, S016, S201. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.

## SKIN-003 — Can stretch marks fade?
- Opening takeaway: Treatments may improve existing marks, but complete removal is not guaranteed.
- Visual sequence: Show variation in color and texture without idealized before/after comparisons.
- Closing action: Consider a dermatologist after recovery if the marks bother you.
- Always visible: Defer elective procedures during pregnancy. Later assessment must account for healing, site, skin type and lactation. RF microneedling has a device-specific FDA harm warning.
- Sources: S018, S019, S020, S024, S027, S162, S198, S216. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.

## ABD-001 — Will my core recover?
- Opening takeaway: Strength can improve even when the abdominal gap stays similar.
- Visual sequence: Show abdominal muscles and connective tissue; label width and function as different measures.
- Closing action: Discuss gradual strengthening after birth.
- Always visible: The exercise trial does not establish immediate postpartum readiness. Wound problems, pain and pelvic heaviness warrant individual assessment.
- Sources: S028, S030, S031, S032, S033. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.

## ABD-002 — Do belly binders work?
- Opening takeaway: A binder may help early discomfort. Permanent reshaping is unproven.
- Visual sequence: Show a removable support garment versus the underlying abdominal wall.
- Closing action: Ask about support if recovering from cesarean birth.
- Always visible: After cesarean, clinician review of incision, fit and symptoms is appropriate. Rectus diastasis, hernia and wound separation require different evaluations.
- Sources: S035, S036, S038, S039, S041, S207. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.

## BREAST-001 — What changes breast shape?
- Opening takeaway: Pregnancy changes breast tissue. Research does not establish a cosmetic reason to avoid breastfeeding.
- Visual sequence: Show tissue, skin and support structures; never depict one feeding method as cosmetically superior.
- Closing action: Choose comfortable support and the feeding approach you prefer.
- Always visible: Support may be chosen across stages for comfort. A cosmetic procedure during recovery or lactation needs specialist assessment. Do not frame formula, breastfeeding or combined feeding as a cosmetic intervention.
- Sources: S044, S045, S046, S202, S203. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.

- Opening takeaway: Build strength and fitness with habits you can keep.
- Visual sequence: Three simple activity illustrations: strength, aerobic movement and rest.
- Closing action: Choose movement you enjoy and can sustain.
- Always visible: Before conception, avoid imposing trial weight-loss programs on general users. Once pregnant use pregnancy-specific exercise guidance.
- Sources: S049, S050, S052, S055, S056, S059. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.

## FIT-002 — Can I exercise while pregnant?
- Opening takeaway: Activity is generally beneficial in uncomplicated pregnancy, with individual adjustments.
- Visual sequence: Use a stage-based movement illustration with a visible stop-and-seek-care note.
- Closing action: Discuss a comfortable movement plan with your care team.
- Always visible: Across trimesters adapt for comfort, balance and medical conditions. Stop and seek care for bleeding, painful contractions or fluid leakage. No calorie-deficit goal.
- Sources: S057, S058, S059, S062, S187, S188. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.

## FIT-003 — When can I exercise again?
- Opening takeaway: Healing and symptoms matter more than a fixed recovery date.
- Visual sequence: Use a branching recovery path instead of a rigid week-to-week countdown.
- Closing action: Progress gradually and seek assessment for persistent symptoms.
- Always visible: Immediate recovery after uncomplicated vaginal birth differs from major tearing, assisted birth, cesarean or medical complications. Progress individually in every pathway.
- Sources: S064, S066, S067, S068, S173, S196, S211, S218, S221. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.

## PELV-001 — Should I train my pelvic floor?
- Opening takeaway: Both contraction and relaxation matter. Pain needs tailored assessment.
- Visual sequence: Show contract versus release using neutral anatomy; avoid universally prescribing repetitions.
- Closing action: Ask a pelvic-health clinician about technique.
- Always visible: During pregnancy or recovery, supervision is useful for pain or difficulty contracting/relaxing. Discuss contraception before sex if pregnancy is undesired, including while breastfeeding.
- Sources: S069, S070, S071, S072, S193, S194, S200. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.

- Closing action: Discuss late-pregnancy options with your maternity team.

## NUTR-001 — What nutrients matter most?
- Opening takeaway: Folic acid matters before conception. Other nutrient needs depend on your diet and stage.
- Visual sequence: Show the early development timeline and a supplement label with clearly marked units.
- Closing action: Review supplements and their labels with your care team.
- Always visible: Review diet, existing supplements, deficiencies, interactions and overlapping products with the clinician. Avoid excessive preformed vitamin A. Choose lower-mercury fish using FDA species guidance. Review lactation needs separately.
- Sources: S082, S083, S085, S086, S087, S088, S089, S090, S092, S093, S176, S177, S178, S179, S195, S214, S215, S222, S223, S224. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.

## NUTR-002 — Are natural supplements safe?
- Opening takeaway: Evidence varies widely by product, dose and intended benefit.
- Visual sequence: Show a benefit/evidence/safety comparison without shopping buttons.
- Closing action: Discuss concentrated supplements before using them in pregnancy.
- Always visible: Culinary intake differs from concentrated products. Do not infer rare fetal safety from short trials. Report product, timing and symptoms to clinician. Third-party testing addresses aspects of product quality, not clinical benefit.
- Sources: S086, S094, S095, S096, S098, S099, S100, S102, S131, S190, S201. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.

## HAIR-001 — Will my hair grow back?
- Opening takeaway: Postpartum shedding is often temporary, but persistent or unusual loss needs assessment.

## SCAR-001 — When can I care for scars?
- Opening takeaway: Wounds need to heal before cosmetic scar treatments.
- Visual sequence: Show healing versus fully closed scar; separate urgent concerns from later scar care.
- Closing action: Contact maternity care for concerning wound changes.
- Always visible: Immediate postpartum: wound and infection assessment. Following closure: discuss silicone or scar therapy if symptomatic. Delay massage or elective devices until healing is assessed. Severe bleeding or systemic illness needs urgent care.
- Sources: S118, S119, S121, S123, S124, S125, S126, S131, S189, S208. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.

## VEIN-001 — Are swollen legs normal?
- Opening takeaway: Some swelling occurs, but one-sided painful swelling needs urgent assessment.
- Visual sequence: Use separate urgent-warning and ordinary-comfort panels; do not use color alone.
- Closing action: Seek immediate care for one-sided painful swelling; emergency help for chest pain or sudden breathlessness.
- Always visible: Pregnancy and postpartum: urgent symptoms override comfort advice. A stocking is not a diagnostic test or a substitute for prescribed thromboprophylaxis. Later vein procedures require vascular review.
- Sources: S127, S128, S129, S130, S131, S132, S133, S209. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.

## EMERG-001 — What about newer treatments?
- Opening takeaway: A promising mechanism does not prove clinical benefit or safety.
- Visual sequence: Compare intended outcome, evidence and safety; no device settings or procedure demonstrations.
- Closing action: Keep experimental or elective procedures out of routine pregnancy plans.
- Always visible: No home procedure instructions. FDA reports serious injuries with certain RF microneedling uses. Specialist review is needed after healing and during lactation; regulatory clearance depends on device and indication.
- Sources: S162, S163, S164, S165, S166, S167, S168, S186, S198, S199, S210. See SOURCE_REGISTRY.json.
