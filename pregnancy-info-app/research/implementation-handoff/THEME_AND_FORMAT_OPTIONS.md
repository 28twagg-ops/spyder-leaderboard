# Theme and presentation research — options, not final design

No single palette is scientifically proven best for this user. Compare these three intentionally distinct candidate directions, with Claude responsible for final tokens and typography.

| Direction | Surfaces | Text | Accent | Mood hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mineral editorial | #F5F0E7 | #243431 | #38665C, #A65D43 | Warm, grounded and calm |
| Modern anatomical atlas | #F7F7F2 | #222A35 | #355D88, #B96343 | Clear, technical and confident |
| Botanical journal | #F8F4EE | #302A25 | #5A6946, #8F5445 | Natural, personal and approachable |

Use colors as candidate swatches, not accessibility claims. Check each exact foreground/background pairing. WCAG AA normal text 4.5:1, large text 3:1, meaningful non-text controls 3:1, and do not encode risk/evidence with color alone. Target touch controls around 44 CSS px when possible; WCAG 2.2 AA target minimum is 24 CSS px subject to exceptions. Respect reduced motion and avoid flashing. Use diagrams with labels, a narrative first and deep data second. No body-rating or idealized transformations.

Formats to compare: (A) one-screen illustrated brief; (B) short swipeable chapter with one decision; (C) optional 30–90s narrated explanation followed by text; (D) deeper article on demand. Do not autoplay video, force scrolling, or make a carousel the only route. The first 5–15 seconds and 30–60 second session are targets to test, not proven optimal lengths.

Test actual intended user: present three equivalent topic examples with identical content and safety. Counterbalance order, ask them to identify takeaway and action, uncertainty and safety without looking, observe time and misinterpretation, ask which they would voluntarily use again. No testing of actual health status or appearance, no collection in public repo. Use first-click success, comprehension and preference, not time-on-app alone.
