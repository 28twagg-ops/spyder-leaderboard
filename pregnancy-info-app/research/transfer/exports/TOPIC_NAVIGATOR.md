# Topic navigator — research draft

Derived from the current topic bundles, claims and intervention index. Use the source JSON for full evidence, stage-specific qualifiers, latest audit overrides and citations. These are not clinical recommendations. No review or release status is changed.

## SKIN-001 — Can creams prevent stretch marks?

**Takeaway:** Moisturizers can help dryness. Reliable stretch-mark prevention is not established.
Selected source IDs: S001.

**Action:** Moisturize for comfort, not as a required prevention routine.
Selected source IDs: S001.

**Safety:** During pregnancy, check ingredients, especially retinoids. Stop irritating products. Prevention studies give little information about uncommon adverse effects.
Selected source IDs: S204.

Claims: C-SKIN-001, C-O001.
Interventions: INT-001 (Moisturizer); INT-002 (Cocoa butter); INT-003 (Olive oil); INT-004 (Shea butter); INT-005 (Vitamin E topical); INT-006 (Centella-containing cream); INT-007 (Hyaluronic acid cream); INT-008 (Bitter almond oil with massage).
Phase IDs: none recorded.

## SKIN-002 — Is collagen worth it?

**Takeaway:** Adult skin studies disagree. Pregnancy stretch-mark prevention is unproven.
Selected source IDs: S011, S012.

**Action:** Keep collagen off the required checklist.
Selected source IDs: S011.

**Safety:** Before conception, adult cosmetic findings apply only to similar populations. During pregnancy and lactation, product-specific safety and benefit for this indication remain unestablished.
Selected source IDs: N323, N324.

Claims: C-SKIN-002.
Interventions: INT-009 (Oral collagen peptides).
Phase IDs: none recorded.

## SKIN-003 — Can stretch marks fade?

**Takeaway:** Treatments may improve existing marks, but complete removal is not guaranteed.
Selected source IDs: S106, S018.

**Action:** Consider a dermatologist after recovery if the marks bother you.
Selected source IDs: S106, S018.

**Safety:** Defer elective procedures during pregnancy. Later assessment must account for healing, site, skin type and lactation. RF microneedling has a device-specific FDA harm warning.
Selected source IDs: S198, S199.

Claims: C-SKIN-003.
Interventions: INT-010 (Conventional microneedling); INT-011 (Fractional nonablative laser); INT-012 (Fractional CO2 laser); INT-013 (Topical tretinoin for striae).
Phase IDs: P22.

## BREAST-001 — What changes breast shape?

## NUTR-002 — Are natural supplements safe?

**Takeaway:** Evidence varies widely by product, dose and intended benefit.
Selected source IDs: S094, S095.

**Action:** Discuss concentrated supplements before using them in pregnancy.
Selected source IDs: N319, N320.

**Safety:** Culinary intake differs from concentrated products. Do not infer rare fetal safety from short trials. Report product, timing and symptoms to clinician. Third-party testing addresses aspects of product quality, not clinical benefit.
Selected source IDs: N319, N320.

Claims: C-NUTR-002, C-O015, C-O016, C-O017, C-O018.
Interventions: INT-058 (Ginger); INT-059 (Probiotic supplements); INT-060 (Creatine); INT-061 (Third-party supplement testing).
Phase IDs: P09.
