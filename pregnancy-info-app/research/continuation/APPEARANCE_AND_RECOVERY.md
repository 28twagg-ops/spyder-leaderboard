# Appearance and recovery — selected follow-up checks

## SKIN-001: Genetic predisposition
A 2025 prospective multicenter cohort of 1,017 Han Chinese pregnant participants reported striae in 59%. Family history, adolescent striae, age, skin type and pre-pregnancy BMI were associated with the outcome; genotype associations require replication. The authors declared no competing interests; registration CTR2300077737. [Xiong et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41083953/), DOI 10.1186/s12884-025-08144-4. A 2013 GWAS also examined a separate cohort of 4,967 European-descent 23andMe customers reporting pregnancy stretch-mark severity. [GWAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3806248/). Commercial recruitment, self-report, ancestry and observational design limit generalization. These are not intervention trials and cannot establish that changing weight or supplements prevents striae. Avoid personal genetic risk scores. Diagram: genetic/hormonal and mechanical influences; label prevalence as cohort-specific.

A topical prevention review and Mayo Clinic guidance do not establish a reliable preventive benefit for common oils/creams. [Review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25255817/), [Mayo](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/stretch-marks/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20351144). Shea-only and vitamin E-only pregnancy trial evidence remains unestablished in this targeted search; no proof of ineffectiveness for an isolated ingredient was established. Record comfort separately from prevention.

## ABD-001/002: Exercise, binders and abdominal appearance
A 2023 synthesis reported six trials, 161 participants, mean difference in postpartum inter-recti distance −0.43 cm (95% CI −0.82 to −0.05) versus usual care, moderate certainty. [Review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36934466/). A 2025 network meta-analysis identifies comprehensive muscle exercise, breathing and NMES combinations as promising for distance; indirect comparisons and outcome definitions must be considered. [Review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41224804/). A 2025 exercise-vs-binding review reports different effects above and below the umbilicus; do not combine site-specific values until units and measurement procedures are verified from full text. [Review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40018828/). A 2026 updated review exists [PMID 41995773](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41995773/) but full result extraction was blocked in this pass; baseline S030 already includes it. Do not rank therapies based only on abstracts.

Quick view: recovery of strength, pain and daily function matters separately from width of the gap; a narrower gap does not guarantee appearance changes. No universal exercise prescription or NMES use in pregnancy. Refer persistent symptoms, suspected hernia or complex rehabilitation for assessment.

## FIT-003: Return to activity
The 2025 Canadian postpartum movement guideline ties progression to healing and symptoms; return to resistance training/running depends on recovery and bleeding response. [Guideline](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/59/8/515/). Two 2024 Delphi consensus papers support individualized readiness screening, gradual loading and walk-run progression; these are expert consensus, not randomized proof of injury prevention. [Readiness](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38148108/), [program design](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38191239/). Avoid turning any minimum elapsed time into automatic clearance. Prospective injury rates by delivery, lactation and training subgroup remain unextracted.

## FACE-001: Tinted sunscreen
A prospective randomized comparative melasma relapse study compared UV protection with added short-wavelength visible-light protection. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25443629/). This is indirect evidence for preventing first-onset melasma in pregnancy; numerical outcomes, product details, funding and pregnancy applicability need full extraction. Do not imply a specific cosmetic formulation is necessary for everyone.

## EMERG-001/SKIN-003: Radiofrequency microneedling
FDA's 15 October 2025 safety communication describes reported serious complications from certain aesthetic RF microneedling uses, including burns, scarring, fat loss, nerve injury and disfigurement. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/safety-communications/potential-risks-certain-uses-radiofrequency-rf-microneedling-fda-safety-communication) (already S198). These are adverse-event signals without a population denominator and should never become a complication-rate chart. Device clearance is indication-specific; not pregnancy safety approval. No at-home RF microneedling or prenatal cosmetic task. If considering later treatment, seek an appropriately trained licensed clinician and compare alternatives. Skin-type-specific outcomes and long-term durability need further appraisal.
