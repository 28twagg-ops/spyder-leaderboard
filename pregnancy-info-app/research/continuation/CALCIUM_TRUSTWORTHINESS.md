# NUTR-001 — Calcium and preeclampsia: trial integrity disagreement

## Quick takeaway
Meeting calcium needs is distinct from taking high-dose supplements to prevent preeclampsia. Guidelines for low-intake populations and recent evidence reviews disagree. Discuss supplemental treatment with maternity care.

| Source | Evidence |
|---|---|
| WHO context-specific recommendation S224 | Low dietary calcium populations; 1.5–2.0 g elemental calcium/day during pregnancy in the cited guidance, not a blanket individual prescription |
| Cochrane 2025 S092 | Six RCTs, 15,364 women; RR 0.83 (0.67–1.04); low certainty; RD 9 fewer per 1000 (17 fewer to 2 more) |
| 2026 methodological commentary S093 | Sensitivity analysis adding two disputed trials: eight studies, 15,814 women; RR 0.63 (0.45–0.90) |

Sources: [WHO compiled guideline](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615641/table/part1.tab212/), [Cochrane 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001059.pub6), [2026 commentary and Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13312738/).

The updated review removed 20 previously included trials: 11 for changed eligibility and nine pending trustworthiness classification. The commentary challenges how the screening tool was applied. In Lopez-Jaramillo 1997 (Ecuadorian adolescents), the commentary reports 4/125 vs 21/135 cases, RR 0.21 (0.07–0.58), and argues that low dietary calcium, age, altitude and nulliparity make the observed control rate plausible. In Purwar 1996, RR 0.17 (0.04–0.77); the commentary identifies reported ethics approval and computer-generated randomization. This is secondary extraction from commentary, not independent verification of original trial reports.

The commentary notes that another Cochrane review using a different trustworthiness checklist accepted these and two other disputed trials. Screening differences are a plausible explanation for changed conclusions. The commentary's alternative analysis is nested within the main analysis and is not independent evidence. Neither concern nor an author rebuttal alone establishes a trial trustworthy or fraudulent. The 2025 review was partly WHO-funded; full commentary and trial disclosures require extraction.

Show both sensitivity scenarios in a single comparison panel. Avoid suggesting the results are two independent scientific votes. Recheck guidance at release; distinguish adequate intake, prevention in low-intake populations and treating established disease. A separate 2025 [preconception review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40965861/) drew on one trial and does not establish a years-ahead supplementation strategy. No preconception high-dose action.
