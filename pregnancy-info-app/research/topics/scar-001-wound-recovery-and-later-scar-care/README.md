# Wound recovery and later scar care

**A wound opening needs maternity care. Cosmetic scar care comes after healing.**

**Safety by stage.** Immediate postpartum: wound and infection assessment. Following closure: discuss silicone or scar therapy if symptomatic. Delay massage or elective devices until healing is assessed. Severe bleeding or systemic illness needs urgent care.

## Practical choice

Contact maternity care for wound separation, new bleeding, discharge, worsening pain or feeling unwell. Discuss scar appearance after the wound has closed.

## Effort and cost

In T118, participants used assigned dressings for three months. Costs depend on dressing dimensions and changes.

## What the research measured

Cesarean incisions and repaired perineal tears have different anatomy and infection risks. The PREVIEW pilot compared resuturing with expectant management of dehisced perineal wounds. Silicone trials usually study closed scars or hypertrophic scars. Their results cannot direct treatment of an open wound.

Guideline-supported wound assessment. Low or very low certainty for many silicone outcomes; comparator, scar age and measurement scale matter. Pilot resuturing findings require larger trials.

- **Split-incision randomized comparison** · Horizontal cesarean incision. Silicone sheet vs paper tape on opposite halves for three months. Patient-rated appearance favored silicone at 6 and 12 months; Vancouver Scar Scale differences were unclear. Paired halves, different outcome instruments; clinical importance uncertain. [S118](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32427792/)
- **Pilot feasibility RCT** · Dehisced perineal wound after vaginal birth. Resuturing vs expectant management. Early healing favored resuturing; by 6–8 weeks all but one wound had healed. Few events and very wide interval; strong treatment preferences affected recruitment. [S124](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/5306527/)
