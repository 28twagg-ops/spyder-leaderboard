# Back, pelvic girdle, balance and foot changes

**If walking, turning in bed or climbing stairs hurts, ask for a pelvic-health assessment.**

**Safety by stage.** Antenatal treatment should be adapted to pregnancy by trained clinicians. Persistent postpartum pain or impaired mobility should prompt reassessment; provocative self-testing is unnecessary.

## Practical choice

Discuss individualized physiotherapy, activity modification and a trial of a non-rigid support belt where appropriate. Seek clinical review for new severe or disabling symptoms.

## Effort and cost

The sham-controlled acupuncture trial studied eight weeks. Access and session prices vary.

## What the research measured

Pelvic girdle pain trials differ in diagnosis and comparators. Adding acupuncture to ordinary care tests the whole treatment experience. A sham comparator tests a narrower specific effect. Gait and arch changes are biomechanical outcomes and do not identify who needs treatment.

Low-to-moderate depending on pain outcome and comparator. A longitudinal cohort of 49 completers measured lower average arch height and greater foot length after pregnancy, with individual variation.

- **Longitudinal cohort** · Pregnant participants measured again postpartum. Within-person baseline vs postpartum foot measurements. Mean arch height and rigidity fell and foot length increased. Eleven discontinued. No randomized intervention or individual prediction. [S139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23117270/)
- **Sham-controlled randomized trial** · Pregnant with pelvic girdle pain and baseline pain ≥50/100. Standard care plus acupuncture vs standard care plus nonpenetrating sham for eight weeks. Median pain fell from 66 to 36 and 69 to 41, respectively; between-group P=.493. Sham may have contextual effects; change within either arm does not isolate needling. [S220](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18947338/)
