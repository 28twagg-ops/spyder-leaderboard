# Preventing stretch marks

**No cream reliably prevents pregnancy stretch marks. Moisturizer can be a comfort choice.**

**Safety by stage.** During pregnancy, check ingredients, especially retinoids. Stop irritating products. Prevention studies give little information about uncommon adverse effects.

## Practical choice

Use a tolerated moisturizer if skin feels dry. There is no evidence-based need to start a product years ahead.

## Effort and cost

Daily application until delivery in T004, with no clear prevention benefit. Buying a product solely for prevention is optional.

## What the research measured

Striae involve changes within the dermis. Softening the surface and preventing new dermal marks are different outcomes. Small trials tested finished mixtures, which cannot identify an active ingredient. The review of six trials predates several newer studies.

Low for prevention across different formulations. Cocoa-butter trials were inconclusive. One Centella-containing mixture had a positive trial, while another had a favorable subgroup despite no overall reduction. Massage plus almond oil was studied without secure randomization.

- **Double-blind RCT** · Nulliparous, first trimester. Cocoa butter lotion vs vehicle, daily until delivery. New striae at postpartum assessment: 45.1% vs 48.8%, P=.730. 35 did not complete. Wide uncertainty remains; vehicle may moisturize. [S004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18715434/)
- **Double-blind RCT** · Pregnant participants from 16 weeks. Cocoa butter cream vs placebo cream through delivery. New striae: 44% vs 55%, P=.09. 150 allocated per arm. Verify analyzed denominators before converting percentages to counts. [S008](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0020729209004366)
- **Double-blind trial** · Pregnant participants. Centella-containing combination cream vs vehicle. New striae in 14 treated (34%) and 22 controls (56%). Multiple ingredients; small study and nonstandard severity score. [S009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19291041/)
- **Randomized comparative trial** · Pregnant participants with and without previous striae. Multi-ingredient cream vs base cream. Overall new striae: 37.6% vs 33.3%. In those without previous marks: 5.6% vs 35%. Subgroup precision poor; do not substitute subgroup for overall outcome. [S006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23237514/)
- **Quasi-experimental comparison** · First pregnancy, single center in Turkey. Almond oil plus massage vs oil alone vs usual care. Striae frequencies were 20%, 38.8% and 41.2%, respectively. Groups of 47, 48 and 46. Allocation and co-intervention limit causal attribution. [S010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22594386/)
- **Randomized pregnancy trial** · Nulliparous pregnant participants in Tehran. Olive oil on abdomen without massage vs no topical from enrollment to delivery. No clear difference in incidence or severity of new marks. Small unblinded comparison; baseline susceptibility and adherence matter. [S005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22863639/)
