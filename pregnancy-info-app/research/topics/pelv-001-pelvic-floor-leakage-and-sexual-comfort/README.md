# Pelvic floor, leakage and sexual comfort

**Learning to contract and relax can help. Pain or difficulty relaxing calls for tailored care.**

**Safety by stage.** During pregnancy or recovery, supervision is useful for pain or difficulty contracting/relaxing. Discuss contraception before sex if pregnancy is undesired, including while breastfeeding.

## Practical choice

Ask a pelvic-health clinician about technique. Resume intimacy when wanted and comfortable. Persistent pain, bulging or leakage deserves assessment.

## Effort and cost

T072 involved four months of training. Supervision and therapist access vary. This protocol addressed prolapse in one population.

## What the research measured

Antenatal training for continent participants lowered later reports of leakage. Studies treating established incontinence or prolapse answer different questions. Painful high-tone muscles may require relaxation and coordination. Postpartum intercourse has no universal deadline.

Moderate for prevention of late pregnancy leakage and high for the 3–6 month postpartum outcome in the Cochrane review. Evidence is less certain for treatment, prolapse and later follow-up.

- **Single-blind RCT** · Pregnant participants in antenatal training study. Supervised pelvic floor training vs usual care. Leakage at 36 weeks: 48/148 vs 74/153. At three months postpartum: 29/148 vs 49/153. Self-reported symptoms; keep follow-ups within one study. [S070](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12576255/)
- **Assessor-blinded RCT** · First birth, training began 6–8 weeks postpartum. Four months of pelvic floor training vs control; both taught a correct contraction. No clear difference in prolapse after the intervention. Broad interval. This outcome differs from prevention of leakage. [S072](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24983687/)
