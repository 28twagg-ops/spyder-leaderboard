# Folate, food and prenatal nutrient needs

**Start folic acid before conception. Check what your prenatal actually contains.**

**Safety by stage.** Review diet, existing supplements, deficiencies, interactions and overlapping products with the clinician. Avoid excessive preformed vitamin A. Choose lower-mercury fish using FDA species guidance. Review lactation needs separately.

## Practical choice

For average-risk people who could become pregnant, USPSTF recommends 400–800 micrograms of folic acid daily starting at least one month before conception through the first two to three months of pregnancy. Prior neural-tube defect or certain medicines require an individual plan.

## What the research measured

Neural tube closure occurs early, often before pregnancy recognition. Trials of recurrence prevention enrolled a higher-risk population. A first-occurrence trial used a multivitamin, complicating attribution. Other nutrients serve different roles: iron for red cells, iodine for thyroid hormone, choline for cell membranes, B12 for neurologic function and calcium/vitamin D for bone. Nutrient requirements concern total intake. Extra supplements require evidence for the specific outcome.

High for periconceptional folate and neural-tube defects. Iron studies can show better hematologic indices without resolving population-wide maternal/infant clinical benefit. A newer calcium review challenges older preeclampsia estimates after trustworthiness checks. Omega-3 review estimates and the later ORIP trial differ.

- **Double-blind multicenter RCT** · Pregnancies randomized in Australia; 5,517 individuals. Prenatal n-3 long-chain fatty acids vs control. Birth before 34 weeks: 61/2734 vs 55/2752. Unit is pregnancy. This later trial is independent of the earlier review search. [S082](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1816832)
- **Randomized prevention trial** · People planning pregnancy, primarily first occurrence rather than recurrence. Periconceptional multivitamin vs trace elements. Six neural-tube defects in controls and none in the vitamin group. Known outcomes for 2,104 vitamin and 2,052 control pregnancies; combination formulation. [S087](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1307234/)
- **Multicenter double-blind factorial RCT** · Previous neural-tube defect pregnancy. Folic acid-containing groups vs groups without folic acid. Six affected pregnancies vs 21. Known-outcome completed pregnancies denominator; high baseline recurrence risk. [S089](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/014067369190133A)
