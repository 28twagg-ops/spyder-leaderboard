# Swelling, varicose veins and blood-clot warning signs

**One painful, swollen leg needs urgent assessment during pregnancy or after birth.**

**Safety by stage.** Pregnancy and postpartum: urgent symptoms override comfort advice. A stocking is not a diagnostic test or a substitute for prescribed thromboprophylaxis. Later vein procedures require vascular review.

## Practical choice

Seek immediate care for one-sided swelling, redness or pain. Call emergency services for sudden breathlessness or chest pain. For ordinary leg discomfort, ask whether compression is appropriate.

## Effort and cost

Compression entails fitting and repeat wear. The trial did not establish a price or cost per new vein prevented.

## What the research measured

Pregnancy-associated venous changes can cause discomfort. Compression may change symptoms or venous reflux without preventing new visible veins. Suspected deep vein thrombosis is a separate, urgent condition requiring clinical evaluation.

Limited small-trial evidence for symptom relief. Insufficient evidence for cosmetic prevention. Clinical safety guidance takes priority when symptoms suggest thrombosis.

- **Prospective randomized trial** · Uncomplicated pregnancy, enrolled before 12 weeks. Compression stockings vs no stockings. Leg symptoms improved in 7/27 vs 0/15; no convincing prevention of new visible varicose veins. Small groups; reflux, appearance and comfort are different endpoints. [S127](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11835115/)
