# Oral health and periodontal treatment

**Treat gum and tooth problems for oral health. Preventing preterm birth is a separate claim.**

**Safety by stage.** Preventive, diagnostic and necessary restorative treatment may be provided during pregnancy according to ADA guidance. Individual medications and medical complications need coordination.

## Practical choice

Maintain routine dental care and tell the dental team about pregnancy, medications and symptoms. Needed dental treatment can be arranged during pregnancy.

## Effort and cost

T152 included monthly polishing and oral-hygiene instruction after periodontal treatment. Dental coverage and procedure fees vary.

## What the research measured

Periodontal inflammation has been associated with obstetric outcomes, but common risk factors can explain part of the association. Trials compared antenatal scaling/root planing with delayed treatment. Gum disease improved, while preterm delivery estimates included no effect.

Supported for treating periodontal disease. Larger randomized trials do not establish meaningful obstetric protection. Their intervals leave room for smaller effects.

- **Multicenter RCT** · Periodontitis, randomized at 13–17 weeks. Scaling/root planing before 21 weeks vs after birth. Preterm births: 49/407 vs 52/405; periodontal measures improved. Outcome includes losses as defined by investigators. Distinguish births from live births. [S152](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17079762/)
- **Randomized trial** · Periodontal disease in pregnancy, Western Australia. Periodontal care starting near 20 weeks vs after pregnancy. Preterm birth 9.7% vs 9.3%. Single study across six obstetric sites, no clear obstetric benefit. [S155](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19935025/)
