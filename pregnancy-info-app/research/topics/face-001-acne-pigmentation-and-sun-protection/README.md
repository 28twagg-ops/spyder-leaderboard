# Acne, pigmentation and sun protection

**Protect skin from the sun and check acne products for retinoids when planning pregnancy.**

**Safety by stage.** Review retinoids before trying. Contact the prescriber promptly about any pregnancy exposure, distinguishing oral from topical. Check lactation and infant contact separately for each treatment.

## Practical choice

Review prescription and over-the-counter products. Ask about azelaic acid for acne. Consider shade, protective clothing and an appropriate broad-spectrum sunscreen.

## Effort and cost

Sunscreen use is recurrent. No pooled US cost for a tolerated product was extracted.

## What the research measured

Pigment can respond to sun exposure. Pregnancy-specific evidence for prevention differs from treatment of established melasma in other adults. Observational first-trimester topical retinoid studies address inadvertent exposure and cannot establish the safety of deliberately starting treatment. Oral isotretinoin has its own serious risks.

Guidance supports photoprotection and avoidance of retinoids during pregnancy. Limited observational precision for specific topical compounds, exposure intensity and rare outcomes.

- **Prospective single-arm study with historical comparison** · Pregnant sunscreen users in Morocco. Broad-spectrum sunscreen, historical local estimate as comparator. Five new cases of melasma among 185 completers. Historical incidence is not a concurrent control; no causal risk reduction can be calculated. [S212](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17567299/)
- **Nordic population register cohort** · Infants following first-trimester topical retinoid exposure in prescription registers. Topical retinoids vs unexposed; active comparator analyzed separately. Major congenital malformations in 71/2172 exposed and 111404/3671023 unexposed. Adjusted RR 1.10, 95% CI 0.87 to 1.38. Dispensing is not proof of use. Birth-based ascertainment can miss early pregnancy losses. [S108](https://academic.oup.com/bjd/article/194/4/640/8375699)
