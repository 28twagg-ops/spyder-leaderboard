# Perineal injury, labor support and birth choices

**A chosen support person and a conversation about perineal comfort are reasonable parts of a birth plan.**

**Safety by stage.** Antenatal measures belong in late pregnancy with maternity-team advice. Intrapartum techniques require consent. Compare planned vaginal, assisted and cesarean pathways with individual clinical indications and preferences.

## Practical choice

Discuss late-pregnancy perineal massage, optional warm compresses during pushing, preferred support and birth position. Preferences can change during labor.

## Effort and cost

S170 describes antenatal trials beginning around week 35, including schedules as infrequent as once or twice weekly. Continuous support requires a person available through labor.

## What the research measured

Late-pregnancy massage reduced suturing in pooled trials, especially among people without prior vaginal birth. Warm compresses during second stage probably reduce severe tears. Continuous support trials measure experience as well as mode of birth. Epidural use and parity change position evidence.

Moderate for severe tears with warm compresses. Lower certainty for many other intrapartum techniques and continuous-support outcomes. Less suturing does not guarantee an intact perineum.

- **Pragmatic multicenter RCT** · First birth, singleton cephalic at term, epidural, second stage. Upright vs lying down position. Spontaneous vaginal birth was more frequent in the lying-down group. Open label; cannot generalize to birth without epidural. [S080](https://www.bmj.com/content/359/bmj.j4471)

## Educational illustration

Contrast antenatal massage with intrapartum compresses. Plot severe tears, episiotomy and suturing on distinct rows.
