# Reading a prenatal label

**Food and supplements both count toward most daily nutrient targets.** A bottle can omit a nutrient or list an amount well below the total-intake reference. A diet and medication review helps identify a genuine shortfall. The following US values apply to pregnant adults aged 19–50. An RDA is a recommended dietary allowance. An AI is an adequate intake used when an RDA cannot be established. [S086](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Pregnancy-HealthProfessional/)

| Nutrient | Daily total | Reference |
|---|---:|---|
| Calcium | 1000 mg | RDA |
| Choline | 450 mg | AI |
| Folate | 600 mcg DFE | RDA |
| Iodine | 220 mcg | RDA |
| Iron | 27 mg | RDA |
| Magnesium (ages 19–30) | 350 mg | RDA |
| Magnesium (ages 31–50) | 360 mg | RDA |
| Omega-3 as ALA | 1.4 g | AI |
| Vitamin B12 | 2.6 mcg | RDA |
| Vitamin C | 85 mg | RDA |
| Vitamin D | 15 mcg | RDA |
| Zinc | 11 mg | RDA |

**Folate units matter.** The 600 mcg dietary folate equivalent target and the 400–800 mcg folic acid prevention recommendation use different units and answer different questions. Do not add those numbers. [S084](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/folate-healthprofessional/) [S088](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/folic-acid-for-the-prevention-of-neural-tube-defects-preventive-medication)

**Iron.** WHO recommends routine antenatal iron and folic acid in its global public-health guidance. The USPSTF found inadequate evidence to judge screening or routine iron supplementation for maternal and infant health outcomes in asymptomatic pregnant people in its US preventive-care framework. Preventing laboratory anemia, improving clinical outcomes and treating diagnosed deficiency are different indications. Use local prenatal guidance and individual assessment. [S178](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/iron-deficiency-anemia-in-pregnant-women-screening-and-supplementation), [S179](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/index.php/document/final-evidence-summary/iron-deficiency-anemia-in-pregnant-women-screening-and-supplementation), [S222](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11325660/), [S223](https://www.who.int/tools/elena/interventions/daily-iron-pregnancy)

**Calcium.** WHO guidance for low-intake populations predates the 2025 Cochrane reassessment. The latter found little or no reduction in preeclampsia in sensitivity analyses of large trials. A subsequent commentary challenges trustworthiness-based exclusions and calls for trial-level sensitivity analysis. Record publication date, baseline intake and indication before conveying an action. [S092](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD001059_taking-calcium-supplements-during-pregnancy-effective-way-prevent-blood-pressure-disorders-and), [S093](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13312738/), [S224](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615641/table/part1.tab212/)

**Food, supplements and lactation.** FDA fish guidance uses species and serving frequency. ALA is distinct from DHA/EPA. Vegan eating patterns warrant particular attention to B12 and iodine. During lactation the CDC gives different iodine and choline targets. High-dose vitamins are separate interventions from meeting requirements. For pregnancy protein, fiber and total water, record food and beverage contributions and individualized needs; obtain current source tables before adding personalized calculations. [S086](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Pregnancy-HealthProfessional/), [S176](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish), [S177](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/healthy-eating-during-pregnancy), [S195](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10925/chapter/25), [S214](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD004072_vitamin-c-supplementation-pregnancy), [S215](https://www.cdc.gov/breastfeeding-special-circumstances/hcp/diet-micronutrients/maternal-diet.html)
