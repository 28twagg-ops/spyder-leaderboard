# Plain-language and funding transparency requirements
User-approved 2026-11-26. Applies to all past and future research stages and the application. Claude owns actual UI design.
Quick view: aim for US grade 7–8 comprehension (approximately age 13), 1–2 short sentences, concrete action or uncertainty, necessary safety visible. Learn more: straightforward language, define unfamiliar terms inline, preserve population and limitations. Research view: exact numbers, study design, dates, source links and funding; explain units, denominator, baseline, uncertainty and whether the finding is causal. Reading-level tools are screening aids, not proof of comprehension; test with representative readers. Never remove clinical qualifiers to improve a readability score.
Every topic: source-linked funding and bias drawer where material; optional compact statistic if valid, not decorative. Show distinct measures: funding/financial ties (direct grants, in-kind, employer, consulting, patents, honoraria, intermediary/ultimate funder, sponsor roles); methodological risk of bias as judged with named tool; and verification completeness. Show source date, scope, numerator, denominator, denominator type (unique trials vs reports vs claim citations), missing/unknown, grouping rules and limitations. Do not describe percent sponsored as percent biased. Do not infer corporate ties from a name alone; distinguish public foundation, corporate foundation, trade association, and direct commercial manufacturer. Track author affiliations and disclosures as of publication and separately later disclosures with date. A named-company percentage requires verified numerator across a defined reviewed universe; multi-company categories may overlap and must be labeled. No invented 0% when not assessed. Counts are not meta-analysis weights; prioritize comparable independently replicated results, report independent-only and full estimates when legitimate, never automatically discard negative industry-funded results or assume independent claims are valid. Record review funding separately from primary trial funding. Include source-level links and conflicts that could alter findings, not accusations of fraud. For 0–1 studies, use plain disclosure rather than a pie chart. Cite original article/corrected supplement. 
Use absolute counts and understandable units first, e.g. 10 out of 100 vs 15 out of 100; define risk difference as extra/fewer people out of 100. A standardized score is not a percent or an IQ gain; correlation is not causation. Report confidence intervals as uncertainty ranges, not individual probabilities. Provide textual alternatives to all charts; do not use color alone. No scores of parental success, body appearance or child intelligence. Never hide conflicting studies or safety. 
Acceptance: each published quick card linked to same evidence and funding records as detail view; verify scope and arithmetic, unknown treatment, no duplicate cohorts, source links, legibility with large text, and safety visible on first screen. All medical copy remains draft pending qualified review.
