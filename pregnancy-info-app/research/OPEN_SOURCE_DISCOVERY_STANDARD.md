# Open-source discovery and bias appraisal standard
Owner directive 2026-11-27. Supplements INDEPENDENT_EVIDENCE_REVIEW.md and EVIDENCE_GRADING.md; all medical safety holds remain in force.

## Principle
Do not confuse conventional wisdom, authority, peer review, independence, popularity, or contrarianism with validity. No source can be certified entirely unbiased. Investigate claims using original data, transparent methods, directness, reproducibility, competing explanations, harms and funding. Apply equal standards to natural, traditional, commercial and conventional interventions. Prefer strong direct independent replication; do not discard credible evidence solely because it is sponsored or accept poorly designed work solely because it is independent.

## Discovery lanes
1. Scientific literature: PubMed/Europe PMC, Cochrane, Crossref, relevant specialist journals and repositories; multilingual and non-US searches where appropriate. Search for benefit, null, harm, retraction, correction, competing mechanisms and alternative intervention names.
2. Grey literature: ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP, protocols and unpublished/terminated trials; theses/dissertations, institutional repositories, conference abstracts, regulatory assessment reports and clinical study reports; preprints with version history. Evaluate empirical methods even if not peer-reviewed, mark version/status and limitations; distinguish registration from results. Link multiple reports to the same underlying study.
3. Community/qualitative: podcasts, books, patient narratives, traditional practices, forums, documentaries, advocacy, midwife/physio and practitioner accounts, social media. Record creator, exact timestamp/page, date, direct source, claim and financial/advocacy interests. Capture experiences, access barriers, underresearched outcomes, questions and possible harm signals. Trace cited papers, identify original dataset if any. An account may support that person's experience, not population efficacy or causality. Do not mine identifiable private data, quote beyond rights, infer demographics or inflate anecdote counts.
4. Regulatory and product safety: labels/clearances for status and warnings, device recalls, spontaneous reports for signals, not causal or incidence estimates; pricing and marketing for market context only. FDA AEMS reports cannot establish incidence or causation. Compare primary data and current guidance; verify marketed product and country. Do not use retailer ratings as clinical evidence.

## Claim workflow
- Define neutral question, population/stage, intervention, comparator, outcomes, timing and safety. Predefine inclusion/exclusion and search across lanes; log dates, queries, databases, languages, deduplication and screening reasons. No invented PRISMA or completeness claims.
- Record source and underlying study IDs; title/date/URL/DOI/PMID/registry; exact page/table/time; source type/status; original evidence vs hearsay; access/version; funding, direct/indirect interests and sponsor control; methods, denominator, effect, CI, harms, missingness, confounding, applicability; corrections/retractions and replication.
- Risk of bias separately from funding: randomization/blinding where appropriate, selection, measurement, missingness, selective reporting, confounders, publication bias and conflicts. Use appropriate tools (RoB2, ROBINS-I, AMSTAR2 where applicable), outcome-specific GRADE-style certainty, not arbitrary numeric independence weights. Log uncertainty and conflicting findings even when inconvenient; avoid interpreting a non-significant result as proof of equivalence.
- Create independent interpretation BEFORE comparison to historical AI synthesis where possible; log any prior exposure and disagreements. Label as verified original extraction, preliminary, unverified lead, contradicted, held, or clinically reviewed as appropriate. A peer-reviewed paper can be weak; a preprint can be rigorous but unreviewed. Check exact version and compare later changes. No publication-status shortcut.
- For each user-visible claim, show what we know, what is contested, why it matters, limits/harms, study design and direct original links. Separate qualitative experience from comparative quantitative evidence. Do not present unreviewed personal testimony as a treatment protocol or recommendation. No invented quantitative pain/health/sensation scoring or recommendations solely from mechanistic rationale. Do not suppress established safety advice or delay care based on unverified claims.
- Maintain a media lead ledger (ID, URL, timestamp, title, author, claim, provenance, declared interests, cited original sources, screening status, reasons, evidence linkage, consent/rights, review date); keep hypotheses outside clinical charts until eligible. Human clinical review of exact wording and release permission remain distinct from scientific appraisal. No unlicensed asset rehosting.
