# High-effort targeted evidence audit — 2026-11-26

Scope: indexed primary/review excerpts, selected full-text sections, and official tables. Not a systematic review or exhaustive clinical appraisal. All conclusions limited to population, comparator and outcome. Source labels link to original publications.

## HA-001 — NUTR-002
SPRING randomized overweight or obese pregnant participants in Brisbane. GG plus BB-12 strains; preeclampsia secondary outcome, 9.2% vs 4.9%, p=.09. NHMRC and hospital foundation support reported; Chr Hansen supplied active/placebo products in same-cohort report.

**Limitations:** Primary abstract supports percentages, not complete PE adjudication or funding extraction; do not count secondary reports as new trials.

**Implication:** Show commercial in-kind support separately from grant funding.

Sources: [R001: SPRING trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30659070/); [H001: SPRING study protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3585705/); [H002: SPRING secondary microbiome analysis and funding](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2210778921000015); [H003: SPRING ketone secondary analysis and product donation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6038017/). Locator: primary abstract; protocol intervention/secondary outcomes; secondary analysis funding/COI.

## HA-002 — NUTR-002
Dublin trial recruited 175 participants with BMI 30–39.9; 138 completed (63 vs75); L. salivarius UCC118 from weeks 24–28. National Maternity Hospital Medical Fund and Ivo Drury Award in conference disclosure.

**Limitations:** Prior Cochrane PE denominators 62/74 differ from completer totals. Do not replace them or transfer funding from Lindsay 2015 GDM treatment trial.

**Implication:** retain research-draft status

Sources: [R002: Lindsay probiotic trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24646819/); [H004: Lindsay 2014 conference report](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-nutrition-society/article/impact-of-probiotics-in-obese-pregnancy-to-reduce-maternal-fasting-glucose-a-randomized-controlled-trial/148C58F917D58383AA680569CC2B76DC). Locator: primary abstract and conference report methods/funding.

## HA-003 — NUTR-002
HUMBA factorial diet/probiotic trial in New Zealand, BMI ≥30, weeks 12–17+6 at randomization; GG and BB12, minimum 6.5 billion CFU in report. Multiple public/charitable/institutional funding sources; Chr Hansen supplied capsules; Roche supplied testing support in protocol.

**Limitations:** Protocol dose differs slightly from report; preeclampsia endpoint omission in broader review unresolved. In-kind support is not proof of biased result.

**Implication:** retain research-draft status

Sources: [R003: HUMBA trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30878323/); [R018: HUMBA publisher report](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0002937819304788); [H031: HUMBA study protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5123375/). Locator: primary abstract; publisher funding; protocol acknowledgments.

## HA-004 — NUTR-002

## HA-005 — NUTR-002
2021 Cochrane narrow review shows PE RR 1.85 (1.04–3.29); 2024 broader review RR 1.14 (.84–1.53), 11 trials 2401. 2022 overweight/obesity review RR 1.91 (1.03–3.55) for PE including superimposed; 2022 broader GDM-prevention review did not identify significant PE effect. New Zealand MARC March 2023 judged causal link not established. McDougall review funded by Gates Foundation and Harry Secomb Foundation.

**Limitations:** Overlapping studies, different inclusion/outcome definitions and certainty; committee assessment predates broader 2024 review. Neither null-significance nor regulatory judgment establishes harmlessness.

**Implication:** Do not present independent votes or universal harm/safety; no routine supplement recommendation for GDM prevention.

Sources: [S094: Probiotics to prevent gestational diabetes mellitus | Cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD009951_probiotiki-dlya-profilaktiki-gestacionnogo-sakharnogo-diabeta); [S095: The effects of probiotics administration during pregnancy on preeclampsia and associated maternal, fetal, and newborn outcomes: a systematic review and meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38447676/); [H029: Probiotics in overweight or obese pregnant women systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35871956/); [H030: 2022 probiotic GDM prevention systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9751955/); [H006: New Zealand Medsafe MARC March 2023 remarks](https://medsafe.govt.nz/profs/PUArticles/June2023/MARCs-remarks-June-2023-meeting.html). Locator: review abstracts, review funding and committee remarks.

## HA-006 — NUTR-001
2025 review RR .83 (.67–1.04), 6 RCTs 15364; critique adds disputed studies, not independent synthesis. Lopez-Jaramillo trial enrolled pregnant teenagers in Ecuador and tested 2 g/day. Purwar abstract reports PE RR .13 (.01–.64), whereas critique secondary extraction .17 (.04–.77); keep both provenance labels and do not silently substitute. WHO convened guideline development meeting Aug 11–12 2026.

**Limitations:** Publisher summary page also exposes obsolete older results; use version-specific DOI. Primary full reports, ethics and denominator reconciliation still outstanding. Meeting announcement is not new final guidance.

**Implication:** Use DOI version and flag conflicting Purwar estimate; do not turn WHO process into new dosing guidance.

Sources: [S092: Is taking calcium supplements during pregnancy an effective way to prevent blood pressure disorders and related problems? | Cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD001059_taking-calcium-supplements-during-pregnancy-effective-way-prevent-blood-pressure-disorders-and); [S093: Trial trustworthiness assessment: commentary on the Cochrane 2025 systematic review on calcium supplementation during pregnancy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13312738/); [H007: WHO guideline development group: calcium supplementation for prevention of pre-eclampsia](https://www.who.int/news-room/articles-detail/guideline-development-group-calcium-supplementation-for-the-prevention-of-pre-eclampsia); [H008: Calcium supplementation and risk of preeclampsia in Ecuadorian pregnant teenagers](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9241285/); [H009: Calcium supplementation and prevention of pregnancy induced hypertension](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8987323/). Locator: 2025 DOI-version abstract; critique Figure 1 text; original abstracts; WHO notice.

## HA-013 — SKIN-001
Specific cream combines hydroxyprolisilane-C, rosehip oil, Centella triterpenes and vitamin E; overall incidence 37.6% treated vs 33.3% control, nonsignificant.

## HA-014 — SKIN-002
2026 technical reanalysis finds inaccurate primary extraction and incompatible measures; dose meta-regression did not establish benefit. Hydration p=.08, elasticity p=.92; heterogeneity 58.8% and 98.6% respectively. Authors report no COI. Proksch 2014: 69 adult women, 23 each across two dose arms and placebo, 8 weeks.

**Limitations:** Reanalysis challenges two identified reviews, not proof all trials invalid. Shared placebo and outcome/timepoint correlation matter. No pregnancy prevention endpoint; complete arm means/SD still pending.

**Implication:** Do not graph funding subgroups as independent estimates or count multiple dose arms as independent controls.

Sources: [S011: Effects of Collagen Supplements on Skin Aging: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0002934325002839); [S012: Oral Collagen Peptides and Skin Rejuvenation: A Systematic Review and an Updated Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43047141/); [CL02: 2026 technical reanalysis of collagen reviews](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.71208); [S015: Oral supplementation of specific collagen peptides has beneficial effects on human skin physiology: a double-blind, placebo-controlled study.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23949208/); [S014: The Oral Intake of Specific Bovine-Derived Bioactive Collagen Peptides Has a Stimulatory Effect on Dermal Matrix Synthesis and Improves Various Clinical Skin Parameters](https://doi.org/10.3390%2Fcosmetics12020079). Locator: 2025 review abstract; 2026 review publisher excerpt; Grimes/Mahto abstract and Fig 1; primary trial abstract.

## HA-015 — SKIN-002

## HA-016 — ABD-001

## HA-018 — PELV-001
OASI rehabilitation is a separate population. Systematic review describes substantial dyspareunia and sexual dysfunction after severe injury; did not establish significant physical-therapy impact on sexual dysfunction. Observational comparison cannot establish causality.

**Limitations:** Bladder/bowel symptoms, quality of life and sexual function are separate endpoints. No universal exercise protocol.

**Implication:** Offer assessment and symptom-based care; no deadline for resuming sex.

## HA-019 — FACE-001
4% niacinamide vs 4% hydroquinone trial in melasma is not a pregnancy prevention study. Review describes niacinamide as pregnancy/lactation compatible but direct pregnancy clinical outcome evidence remains limited.

**Limitations:** Adult treatment cannot establish prevention or uncommon fetal safety; hydroquinone comparator is not pregnancy recommendation.

**Implication:** Label treatment vs prevention; review formulation with clinician.

Sources: [H019: Double-blind randomized trial of niacinamide 4% vs hydroquinone 4% for melasma](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3142702/); [H020: Update on Melasma—Part II: Treatment](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13555-022-00780-4); [R014: Visible light protection and melasma relapse](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25443629/); [S212: Evaluation of the effectiveness of a broad-spectrum sunscreen in the prevention of chloasma in pregnant women](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17567299/). ## HA-028 — FIT-002

## HA-029 — SKIN-003
Small microneedling comparison follows early and mature striae to six months. Separate 32-patient microneedling study reports 7 pigment changes and 1 infection (25% overall complications). In a fractional CO2 laser plus topical treatment trial, pigment change was reported in 95.8% of participants, decreasing by six months.

**Limitations:** Different devices, populations and ascertainment; rates not comparable head-to-head. Long-term follow-up beyond one year and pregnancy-specific safety unestablished.

**Implication:** Display pigment/infection risks and skin-type context; do not present these rates as general risks.

## HA-030 — ABD-002

## HA-031 — VEIN-001
60 pregnant participants randomized 30 per group in stocking trial; intervention group had smaller calf and ankle diameter increases. Earlier trial did not establish prevention of new varicose veins.

**Limitations:** Edema circumference and cosmetic vein formation differ; adherence and uncommon harms not fully appraised.

**Implication:** Separate symptom relief from cosmetic prevention and urgent clot symptoms.

Sources: [S130: Positive perception and efficacy of compression stockings for prevention of lower limb edema in pregnant women](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35399341/); [S127: Compression stockings prophylaxis of emergent varicose veins in pregnancy: a prospective randomised controlled study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11835115/). ## HA-032 — BIRTH-001
EVA trial 717 randomized, 702 primary analysis; lateral episiotomy 21/344 OASI versus 47/358 no episiotomy, RR .46 (96% CI .28–.78). Wound infection and dehiscence more common in intervention arm. US observational register comparison shows association of operative delivery with higher OASI odds.

**Limitations:** Specific nulliparous patients needing vacuum extraction; cannot extend to spontaneous vaginal delivery or select delivery mode based on observational odds. Interim analysis requires 96% CI.

**Implication:** Present benefit and wound tradeoffs with consent; not a universal episiotomy recommendation.

Sources: [H040: EVA trial: lateral episiotomy or no episiotomy in vacuum assisted delivery](https://www.bmj.com/content/385/bmj-2023-079014); [H041: Contemporary trends in operative vaginal delivery and obstetric anal sphincter injuries 2016–2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40959760/). Locator: EVA primary abstract and indexed table; register study abstract.

## HA-033 — MSK-001
Consensus includes ergonomics, education, strengthening, psychosocial factors and support aids. Trial found rigid/nonrigid belts did not add to exercise and advice in studied population. Recent review rates exercise evidence for medium-term pain/function as low certainty.

**Limitations:** Consensus is not proof of efficacy; belt outcomes and diagnoses differ; no individual treatment or ergonomics protocol established.

**Implication:** Keep support belts optional and symptom-specific; no permanent posture correction claims.

Sources: [H042: Management of pregnancy-related pelvic girdle pain: international Delphi study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35202976/); [H043: Management of symphysis pubis dysfunction during pregnancy using exercise and pelvic support belts](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16305268/); [H052: Effectiveness of physiotherapy interventions for pregnancy-related lumbopelvic pain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42431130/). Locator: consensus abstract; trial conclusion; review conclusion.

## HA-034 — REPRO-001
US vaccine guidance is stage-, product-, risk- and season-specific; carrier screening ideally discussed before pregnancy to allow informed reproductive choices.

**Limitations:** CDC schedule and product recommendations may change; individual eligibility not established. No automated vaccination or testing advice.

**Implication:** Use current official links and clinician discussion rather than static schedule three years ahead.

Sources: [S171: Vaccine Recommendations Before, During, and After Pregnancy | Pregnancy & Vaccines | CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines-pregnancy/recommended-vaccines/); [H044: CDC guidelines for vaccinating pregnant women](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines-pregnancy/hcp/vaccination-guidelines/); [H045: ACOG carrier screening for genetic conditions](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/03/carrier-screening-for-genetic-conditions); [S142: Prepregnancy Counseling | ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2019/01/prepregnancy-counseling). Locator: CDC current public page and vaccination guidelines; ACOG preconception and carrier guidance.

## HA-035 — FIT-001
Iran couples education RCT (168 couples) tested four weekly sessions; male preconception education may improve knowledge/behaviors. Scoping review identifies low awareness and sparse intervention research.

**Limitations:** Educational outcomes do not demonstrate higher live birth; infertility-specific lifestyle evidence cannot be applied to all couples.

**Implication:** Partner education and general health habits, not speculative supplements or fertility guarantees.

Sources: [H046: Online education of men's reproductive health before childbearing: randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40575499/); [H047: Preconception care for men: scoping review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41558839/); [S051: Preconception lifestyle advice for people with infertility](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8092458/). Locator: trial abstract; scoping review abstract; Cochrane review.

## HA-036 — WELL-001
Group parenting intervention in fathers with postpartum depression reported improved depression at four months (group difference ratio .66, 95% CI .47–.91) and other outcomes.

**Limitations:** Selected symptomatic fathers; treatment rather than universal prevention; design, population and outcome scale need full extraction.

**Implication:** Include partner mental health as optional support pathway; no universal program or diagnostic score.

Sources: [H048: Group parenting intervention for male postpartum depression: cluster RCT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39356541/). Locator: cluster RCT abstract.

## HA-037 — DENT-001
Updated systematic review reports PTB RR .85 (.71–1.02), moderate certainty as graded by authors; absolute estimate 20 fewer per 1000 (39 fewer to 3 more).

**Limitations:** CI spans no effect; heterogeneity, disease severity and overlap not fully extracted. Earlier larger RCTs did not establish obstetric benefit.

**Implication:** Maintain dental care for oral health, acknowledge possible but uncertain obstetric benefit.

Sources: [S154: Effect of dental treatments on reduction of preterm birth: a systematic review and meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajogmf.2025.101884); [S152: Treatment of periodontal disease and the risk of preterm birth.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17079762/); [S155: Treatment of periodontal disease during pregnancy: a randomized controlled trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19935025/). Locator: updated review abstract and earlier trial context.

## HA-038 — FIT-003
Prior injury, novice status, fatigue and incontinence associated with running pain in selected postpartum runners; this is not prospective injury incidence.

**Limitations:** Self-selection and confounding; do not use regression odds as an individual injury prediction or show automatic readiness score.

**Implication:** Continue symptom-based, individualized progression.

Sources: [S218: What are the biopsychosocial risk factors associated with pain in postpartum runners? Development of a clinical decision tool](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8360599/); [S067: Clinical and exercise professional opinion on designing a postpartum return-to-running training programme: an international Delphi study and consensus statement](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-107490). Locator: postpartum runner survey abstract; consensus.
