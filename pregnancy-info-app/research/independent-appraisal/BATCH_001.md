## Question 1 — What can randomized trials establish about periconceptional vitamin interventions and neural tube defects?

### S087 — Czeizel & Dudás, NEJM 1992
- Original: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1307234/ ; DOI 10.1056/NEJM199212243272602. Publisher https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM199212243272602 (full-page fetch blocked). Locator: indexed primary abstract, Methods/Results; date above.
- Design: randomized Hungarian first-occurrence prevention trial comparing a multivitamin/mineral preparation including folic acid with a trace-element comparison preparation. Most participants planning a first pregnancy. Confirmed pregnancies 4753; outcomes known in 2104 intervention and 2052 comparator pregnancies. Neural tube defects 0 vs 6 (reported P=.029). Cleft lip/palate not reduced.
- Bias/applicability: randomized design supports causal inference for the tested combination; incomplete known outcomes, rare events and historical population limit precision and generalization. The combination does not isolate folic acid or establish one optimal formulation, modern population absolute benefit or a personal dosing schedule.
- Funding/conflicts: unknown; full declaration inaccessible. No inference from national institute affiliation; direct/in-kind support and sponsor role unknown.
- Conclusion: supports prevention by the tested multivitamin intervention; cannot establish that all ingredients are necessary or that a particular marketed prenatal is superior. Clinical review pending; certainty for broad formulation claims insufficient.

### S089 / P5-02 — MRC Vitamin Study, Lancet 1991
- Original: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1677062/ ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/014067369190133A ; locator primary abstract. Two repository IDs refer to same PMID, not two trials.
- Design: double-blind factorial randomized trial in 1817 participants with previous NTD-affected pregnancy, 1195 completed pregnancies with known defect status. Folic acid factor: 6 NTD cases versus 21 without folic acid; reported RR 0.28 (95% CI 0.12–0.71). Distinguish recurrence prevention from first occurrence. 
- Bias/applicability: factorial comparator and blinding strengthen attribution to folic acid; completed-pregnancy subset and relatively small event count warrant caution. No extrapolation to general population dose, treatment of deficiency or alternative folate formulations. Rare harms cannot be excluded.
- Funding/conflicts: trial name is not proof of sole funding; full original funding, donated materials, commercial ties and sponsor roles not confirmed in this pass. Unknown pending extraction.
- Conclusion: direct randomized recurrence-prevention evidence for folic acid. Complementary to S087, not a basis for individualized dose. No clinical release. 

Independent synthesis: the two trials address different baseline-risk populations and interventions. Do not pool raw event counts or claim equivalent dosing. The direction of evidence supports folic acid prevention, but comparison with other formulations and current clinical guidance requires separate review. This appraisal does not alter established preventive care or existing folate substitution holds.

## Question 2 — Do postpartum curl-up programs narrow diastasis or improve function?

### S028 — Gluppe, Engh & Bø, Journal of Physiotherapy 2023
- Original https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37286390/ ; DOI 10.1016/j.jphys.2023.05.017 ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1836955323000528 ; NCT04122924. Locator primary abstract and indexed publisher Source of support (full page blocked); corroborating investigator conference record https://world.physio/congress-proceeding/effect-home-based-exercise-program-inter-recti-distance-women-diastasis-recti .
- Design: 70 women with DRA 6–12 months postpartum; 12-week exercise program versus no intervention; concealed allocation, assessor blinding, intention-to-treat analysis. Ultrasound primary outcome. Difference in inter-recti distance at rest 2 cm above navel: 1 mm (95% CI −1 to 4). Strength at 10°: 9 Nm (3–16); muscle thickness: 0.7 mm (0.1–1.3). Other secondary effects unclear or small.
- Funding: indexed original article declares full support from Norwegian Women's Public Health Association, H1/2018; investigator conference report concurs. Reported association funding, apparently nonprofit; legal/ultimate funding tracing, in-kind products, author ties, sponsor control and grant terms pending. No assertion of completely independent funding.
- Bias/applicability: no intervention comparator means attention/expectancy differ; sample modest; functional secondary outcomes and multiplicity warrant caution. Findings cannot establish immediate postpartum safety, universal exercise clearance, closure of separation or absence of rare harms. Registry identifier verified in abstract; registration timing and outcome deviations not audited.
- Conclusion: limited evidence of strength/thickness gains without a clear change in measured separation under the studied protocol; neither universal avoidance nor universal prescription supported. No exercise schedule or automated advice.

Context for next batch: independently located earlier randomized postpartum training paper https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5963302/ (different intervention/secondary analysis) and other exercise RCT https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11637234/ ; do not merge without matching populations, protocols, outcomes and cohort identities.

## Question 3 — Does supported baby-led complementary feeding improve growth or change choking risk?

### PED-BLISSGROW — Taylor et al., JAMA Pediatrics 2017
- Original https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/fullarticle/2634362 ; PMID 28692728; DOI 10.1001/jamapediatrics.2017.1284. Locator Results/Table 2, Article Information/Funding/Support/Role of Funder.
- Design: New Zealand RCT of supported modified baby-led feeding (BLISS) versus usual care, 206 participants, 166 retained at 24 months (80.5%). BMI z-score adjusted differences BLISS minus control at 12 months 0.21 (95% CI −0.07 to 0.48) and 24 months 0.16 (−0.13 to 0.45). No clear BMI advantage. Two serious adverse events, one per group, milk choking hospitalizations judged unrelated to intervention.
- Funding: mixed. Article names Lottery Health Research, Meat & Livestock Australia, Karitane Products Society, Perpetual Trustees, New Zealand Federation of Women’s Institutes, University of Otago, in-kind Heinz Watties Ltd, plus Karitane fellowship and university scholarships. Author COI none reported. Authors state funders did not control design, data, analysis, manuscript or submission; contracts not independently examined. Industry/trade and product support must be visible despite author disclosure.
- Bias/applicability: retention and missingness, no participant blinding, intensive support and selected single-region sample limit transfer. Wide intervals do not establish equivalence or long-term weight benefit.
- Conclusion: evidence does not establish BMI improvement or obesity prevention under the tested intervention.

### PED-BLISSCHOKE — Fangupo et al., Pediatrics 2016
- Original indexed abstract https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27647715/ ; publisher https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/138/4/e20160772/52372/A-Baby-Led-Approach-to-Eating-Solids-and-Risk-of ; DOI 10.1542/peds.2016-0772. Full publisher access blocked; abstract/search-indexed disclosure available. Locator Methods/Results.
- 206 infants in same BLISS trial, supported modified feeding vs usual care; 35% reportedly experienced choking between 6–8 months overall; no statistically detectable group difference (reported P>.20 at measured times). Parent reports and calendar/questionnaire measurement limit precision and classification; not evidence of zero risk or equivalence, particularly rare serious events.
- Funding: same trial as PED-BLISSGROW confirmed by registry below. Mixed cohort funding disclosed in growth report; choking paper's complete funding and sponsor-role statements not independently obtained. Indexed publisher author financial disclosure reports no relevant relationships, not proof of no commercial support.
- Conclusion: no detected excess choking with the supported modified approach in this trial, not proof that unsupervised or unmodified baby-led feeding is safe. No relaxation of choking safeguards.

### External candidate PMID 37400061 — Brazilian complementary feeding RCT, 2023
- Original full text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10594018/ ; registry ReBEC RBR-229scm, U1111-1226-9516. Locator Abstract, Methods, Trial registration, Conflicts of interest and Funding.
- Distinct Brazilian three-arm study of caregiver-guided, modified baby-led and mixed approaches with caregiver education; 130 infants followed to 12 months. Reported choking 13/43 (30.2%), 10/45 (22.2%), 11/42 (26.2%), respectively; no significant difference. Does not establish equivalence. Questionnaire-based ascertainment, small groups, caregiver knowledge and COVID-related delivery variation constrain inference.
- Declared funding: FAURGS grant 36908; CNPq 2019-0230; FIPE/HCPA 2019-0230. Authors report no conflicts. Academic/public/hospital funding identified in declaration, no commercial support identified in that statement; other financial ties, indirect funders and sponsor roles not fully traced.
- Conclusion: a distinct corroborating safety comparison with caregiver instruction, not replication of growth endpoint. Candidate external source; no new source registry ID assigned until deduplication/validation.

### Cohort reconciliation and funding comparison
- Registry https://anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=363183 (ACTRN12612001133820) explicitly lists both NZ growth and choking articles among results publications. Same cohort, different outcomes, never independent replications. Other trial reports on iron, zinc, nutrient intake and microbiota require linked-cohort review; registry is not proof every listed article uses identical analysis subsets.
- Supported NZ modified approach with mixed funding and Brazilian trial with academic/public/hospital declared funding both show no statistically detectable choking-group difference. This is qualitative cross-cohort concordance, not equivalence or a sponsorship effect. Different follow-up windows, intervention details, definitions, ascertainment and sample sizes preclude an unadjusted combined estimate. Growth outcome is not independently replicated by Brazilian choking results.
- Unknown and industry-supported findings are not discarded. No claim of a fully unbiased evidence group.
