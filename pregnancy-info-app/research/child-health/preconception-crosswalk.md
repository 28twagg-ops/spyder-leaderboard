# Parental preparation and offspring outcomes

| Question | Existing project records | Outcome and interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Does maternal folic acid timing matter? | DR027, NUTR-001 | Neural tube defects arise early. Prevention guidance starts before conception; a three-year lead time is not established by this evidence. Prior affected pregnancy and certain medications require individualized review. [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/folic-acid/about/) |
| Can additional choline or omega-3 guarantee better cognition? | IA006/IA007; S082/S083 and Gemini audit | Preserve the shared choline cohort, intake comparator, follow-up and attrition. Assess dietary adequacy separately from experimental intake. Separate preterm birth from cognitive endpoints and baseline omega-3 status. |
| Will paternal supplements improve live birth? | IA008, FAZST | Use the trial's infertility-treatment population and live-birth endpoint. Do not transfer paternal folate findings to maternal neural tube prevention or interpret semen quality as future child health. |
| Does exercise before conception improve paternal fertility? | DR021–025, PED-FERTILITY-HOLD | The confirmed dependency affects pregnancy and live-birth estimates in a second synthesis. General fitness, sperm biomarkers, probability of pregnancy and healthy offspring require separate evidence. |
| Is a long preparation period necessary? | family-timeline.csv | The four earlier planning windows provide time for ordinary health care and individual conditions. This targeted review found no direct comparison proving 36 months superior to a shorter preparation period for child development. A condition-specific intervention can have its own evidence-based timing. |
| Can maternal metabolic status predict a child's long-term health? | PED-HAPO | The Hong Kong HAPO follow-up measured associations at about 18 years. Distinguish exposure severity, diagnosis and any subsequent treatment before discussing prevention. |

Next verify parental thyroid status, assisted reproduction/singleton vs multiple gestations, genetic screening, medication-specific washout and occupational exposures. For paternal age, nutrition, spermatogenesis and epigenetics, require endpoint-specific evidence across semen, time to pregnancy, live birth and childhood. A plausible sperm maturation interval alone cannot establish a required intervention duration.
