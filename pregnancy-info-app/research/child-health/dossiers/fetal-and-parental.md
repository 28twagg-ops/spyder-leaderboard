# fetal and parental

Research draft. See the structured claim for stage, certainty and safety exceptions.

## Prenatal sounds

Talking or singing can be a family ritual. The study measured newborn brain responses to sounds.

The exposure groups differed in neonatal responses to speech changes. Later language and intelligence were not measured. No stimulation device or prenatal training regimen follows from EEG results.

**Ask the care team:** What kinds of ordinary sound exposure should we discuss?

[Learning-induced neural plasticity of speech processing before birth](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23980148/)

## Maternal glucose

Discuss glucose screening and follow-up in prenatal care.

A Hong Kong cohort linked higher maternal glucose with some adolescent metabolic outcomes. Shared genetics and postnatal environment can affect both. Observational follow-up cannot predict an individual's future health or prescribe a diet.

**Ask the care team:** How should my screening results be followed up?

[Association between maternal glucose levels in pregnancy and offspring's metabolism and adiposity: an 18-year birth cohort study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40603555/)

## Placental microbiome claims

A placental sequencing result does not justify purchasing a microbiome intervention.

Contamination-controlled tissue analyses attributed most signals to collection, processing or birth. Pathogens can occur in particular cases. No home seeding procedures, probiotic regimens or delivery preferences follow from these data.

**Ask the care team:** Is there a clinical indication for infection testing?

[Human placenta has no microbiome but can contain potential pathogens](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31367035/) · [Risk of Invasive Disease in Preterm Infants Given Probiotics Formulated to Contain Live Bacteria or Yeast | FDA](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/risk-invasive-disease-preterm-infants-given-probiotics-formulated-contain-live-bacteria-or-yeast)

## Cord clamping

Discuss cord clamping with the delivery team, particularly if preterm delivery is expected.

ACOG's 2025 preterm update changes the guidance lineage. Its predecessor also addresses jaundice surveillance in term infants. Hold timing and eligibility logic pending clinician verification of the latest guidance for the birth circumstances.

**Ask the care team:** What circumstances would change the plan at delivery?

[Delayed Umbilical Cord Clamping After Birth: ACOG Committee Opinion, Number 814.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33214530/) · [ACOG Clinical Practice Update: An Update to Clinical Guidance for Delayed Umbilical Cord Clamping After Birth in Preterm Neonates](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40706055/)
