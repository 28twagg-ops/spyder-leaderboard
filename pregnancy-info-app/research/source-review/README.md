## Fresh original-source checks and editorial implications
### P5-05 — Calcium supplementation during pregnancy for preventing hypertensive disorders and related problems
Preeclampsia RR 0.83 (95% CI 0.67–1.04), 6 trials, 15,364 women, low certainty. Clear preventive benefit not established in this analysis. Trustworthiness exclusions materially affect pooled estimates. Calcium adequacy and targeted hypertension prevention differ; do not independently change clinical guidance.

Funding: Review-level and contributing-trial disclosure not fully re-extracted. Interests: Full disclosures pending.

Original: https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001059.pub6 (Updated Cochrane review abstract, search to 7 Jan 2025). Candidate canonical article: EV-ART-009. original abstract checked.

### P5-06 — Trial trustworthiness assessment: commentary on the Cochrane 2025 systematic review on calcium supplementation during pregnancy
Commentary challenges exclusions in the updated review; not a new efficacy trial. Different inclusion decisions need sensitivity analysis. Prior authorship is an intellectual interest, not evidence of distortion.

Funding: No specific grant reported. Interests: Two authors contributed to earlier Cochrane calcium reviews; third declared no competing interests.

Original: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13312738/ (2026 commentary, funding and competing interests). Candidate canonical article: EV-ART-009. selected full-text disclosure passages checked.

### P5-11 — Probiotics to prevent gestational diabetes mellitus
Preeclampsia RR 1.85 (95% CI 1.04–3.29), four trials/955 women; review rated evidence high certainty. Specific pregnancy prevention trials; not evidence about every fermented food, strain or infant population. Preserve review overlap and safety signal.

Funding: Full review and trial funding not newly completed. Interests: Pending full disclosure extraction.

Original: https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD009951_probiotics-prevent-gestational-diabetes-mellitus (Cochrane 2021 results, preeclampsia). Candidate canonical article: EV-ART-009. original review summary checked.

### P2-01 — Breastfeeding and Health Outcomes for Infants and Children
Several favorable child health associations; no clear optimal breastfeeding-duration threshold identified. Certainty differs by endpoint. Most comparisons observational, with residual confounding and heterogeneous feeding definitions.

Funding: AHRQ contract; companion review reports CDC/AHRQ funding. Interests: Full contractor/panel disclosures pending.

Original: https://doi.org/10.23970/AHRQEPCSRBREASTFEEDING (2025 AHRQ report overview and Pediatrics companion review funding). Candidate canonical article: EV-ART-032. original report summary and companion publication excerpt checked.

### P2-02 — Breastfeeding during infancy and neurocognitive function in adolescence: 16-year follow-up of the PROBIT cluster-randomized trial
Randomized breastfeeding promotion, not assigned milk type; existing outcome extraction reports limited verbal-function difference, not broad cognitive advantage. One PROBIT cohort, attrition and cognitive measurement; instrument-company interests relevant.

Funding: CIHR MOP-53155, NIH R01HD050758, public institutional support. Interests: Two authors employed by NeuroTrax, whose cognitive battery was used.

Original: https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1002554 (PLOS funding, competing interests and design; 16-year follow-up). Candidate canonical article: EV-ART-032. selected full-text disclosure and design passages checked.

### PED-PROBIT — Promotion of Breastfeeding Intervention Trial (PROBIT): a randomized trial in the Republic of Belarus
31 Belarus sites assigned to breastfeeding-promotion support or usual care, not breastfeeding versus formula assignment. Cluster randomization and downstream exposure differences; follow-ups are same cohort.

Funding: Original trial disclosure not re-extracted. Interests: Original disclosure pending.

Original: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11242425/ (Original trial abstract, study design). Candidate canonical article: EV-ART-032. original abstract checked.

### PED-BEEP — Daily emollient during infancy for prevention of eczema: the BEEP randomised controlled trial
High-risk term infants: eczema by age two 139/598 versus 150/612; adjusted RR 0.95 (95% CI 0.78–1.16). No clear preventive benefit; skin infection signal reported. High-risk population, specified products and outcome definitions; separate prevention from eczema treatment.

Funding: UK NIHR Health Technology Assessment programme. Interests: Several outside-work industry relationships, institutional Nestlé partnership, and funding-programme affiliations disclosed; investigators affiliated with programme were not involved in its award decision.

Original: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7086156/ (Abstract and declaration of interests). Candidate canonical article: EV-ART-052. selected full-text results and disclosure passages checked.

### PED-CASCADE — Emollients to Prevent Pediatric Eczema: A Randomized Clinical Trial
US pragmatic RCT, 1,247 dyads not selected for high risk: eczema incidence 36.1% vs 43.0% at 24 months, RR 0.84 (95% CI 0.73–0.97). Different risk selection from BEEP, missing-outcome imputation and formulation switching; no basis for brand ranking or universal prevention regimen.

Funding: NIH NIAMS AR071057 and NIH data-platform support. Interests: L'Oreal, Galderma and Pharmaceutical Specialties supplied products; authors report companies not involved in trial design, implementation, analysis or reporting; outside-work industry ties disclosed.

Original: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12287936/ (Abstract, results, funding/support and role of sponsor). Candidate canonical article: EV-ART-052. selected full-text results and disclosures checked.

### PED-PROBIO — Risk of Invasive Disease in Preterm Infants Given Probiotics Formulated to Contain Live Bacteria or Yeast | FDA
FDA warned of potentially fatal invasive infection in preterm infants given live-microbe probiotics and reported an associated death. Regulatory safety warning, not efficacy trial or risk denominator; NICU populations differ from healthy term infants.

Funding: FDA safety communication, not a trial. Interests: Not a study disclosure.

Original: https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/risk-invasive-disease-preterm-infants-given-probiotics-formulated-contain-live-bacteria-or-yeast (FDA 2023 provider safety communication). Candidate canonical article: EV-ART-037, EV-ART-052. original official communication checked.
