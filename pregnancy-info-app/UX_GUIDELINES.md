# UX and visual direction — requirements

Default visits should take 30–60 seconds. The main insight should be understood within 5–15 seconds. Show a maximum of a few meaningful current actions, time/effort, why they matter, and evidence/safety context. Permit skipping, adjusting or disabling actions without guilt. Never score appearance, use punitive streaks or make private photo/measurement tracking default.

## Progressive disclosure
1. Quick visual: a meaningful diagram or graphic, 1–2 sentence takeaway, prominent safety restrictions.
2. Practical: expandable action, timing, frequency where supported, realistic benefit, effort, alternatives and professional involvement.
3. Research: full methodology, directness, results, uncertainty, funding, disagreements and limitations.
4. Sources: citations, original publications and extraction data.
Default quick articles approximately 75–150 words when appropriate; never hide safety or necessary qualifications. Technical terms get accessible definitions. Provide a quick guide and research explorer, with smooth movement between them.

## Visual-first
Use anatomy, skin cross sections, muscle diagrams, timelines, comparison graphics, accessible risk visuals, exercise demonstrations where appropriate, interactive body map and scientific data visualizations. Use licensed or original accurate imagery. No misleading idealized before/after images, unnecessary stock imagery, decorative charts or fabricated transformations. Provide captions, alt text and textual equivalents.

## Distinctive design
Claude chooses name, color, typography, spacing, layout and iconography. Avoid generic AI purple/blue gradients, pastel pink pregnancy themes, excessive round cards, glassmorphism, repeated three-column templates, generic SaaS dashboards and giant repetitive headings. Editorial/scientific field guide, botanical atlas, high-end data journalism and premium journal are optional inspiration. Optional initial palette: slate #222C30, parchment #F1E9D8, brass #C9AA72, eucalyptus #6B8179, clay #A66F58; not mandatory. Distinct layouts for timeline, journal, scientific article, body atlas and comparisons. Light/dark themes, high contrast and accessible semantic colors.

Design mobile-first for one-handed use, low taps, fast loading, readable typography and accessible touch controls. Use representative actual pages to establish the component patterns. Claude documents final decisions in DESIGN_SYSTEM.md and COMPONENT_LIBRARY.md.
