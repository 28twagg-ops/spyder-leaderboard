# Everwell — owner-approved visual journey and editorial content standard
**Controlling experience specification · 2026-11-27 · Version 1.0**

This document preserves the owner-approved interactive chat reference showing Week 22 and Week 23, Mother/Baby selection, a restrained opening, a stage-changing middle, and general reading at the bottom. The owner said this is what we are looking for and requested a thorough written specification as the basis for all remaining weeks and timelines. This is approval of the design direction, not approval of a rendered application, scientific findings, treatment guidance or publication. This document takes precedence over incompatible older layout proposals; the locked design system, medical safety, privacy, evidence, visual-rights and independent-review rules remain controlling.

## 1. Product purpose
Everwell is a visual-first scientific education and preparation companion covering both parents before conception, maternal and fetal development, birth, postpartum recovery, lactation/feeding and child development. Make useful, less commonly discussed research discoverable without obligating the reader to study the literature. Explain biology, possible prevention, comfort, function, nutrition, established care, investigational options and relevant uncertainty. Do not merely reproduce conventional app lists, nor treat novelty or unconventional origin as evidence of efficacy. Avoid optimizing appearance, intelligence, birth timing, or infant behavior as performance targets. Health, autonomy, comfort and informed decisions come first. The interface is an educational reading journey, not a clinician, dosing calculator, diagnosis engine, fetal monitor or intervention scheduler.

The main reading experience answers: What is happening at this stage? Why is it interesting or relevant? What can I reasonably learn or discuss now? What does the evidence actually show? What is not known? What else can I explore? The user should not have to navigate a database to find an answer.

## 3. Screen contract: opening of every pregnancy week
### 3.1 Header and navigation
Use small restrained Everwell wordmark or existing app shell; clear journey back action. Center the week title with a small uppercase trimester context, not a pill. Place previous and next arrows on either side with at least 44px touch targets, clear accessible labels, visible focus, and actual routes. At boundaries disable or direct to the appropriate adjacent life stage. Preserve Mother/Baby selection when switching weeks, including previous/next in the lower page controls where feasible. Keep an accessible choose-week option and avoid exposing a 40-item wall by default. Preserve reading history and back scroll position. Direct links to a particular article must return to the same subject, week and parent topic. Use 40-week reference progress as a navigation indicator, not health score, completion burden or prediction. No forced personal date input; phase selection remains optional and local.

### 3.2 Mother/Baby selector
Place immediately below header/progress, before long content. Two clearly selectable options, with text labels and optional short descriptors: Mother — your body; Baby — development. Switching updates title context, opening illustration, weekly features and relevant ongoing library. Keep selection explicit and visible, not color-only. Respect the user's last selection where appropriate; no needless repeated scroll. Across stages adapt labels (see section 10); do not force fetal visuals into weeks 1–2, or show a pregnant body during preconception/postpartum when inaccurate.

### 3.3 Hero
Compact two-column editorial composition at phone width: left title and brief plain-language introduction, right a meaningful stage/subject illustration. Approximately one short paragraph, not an article. On very small screens/large text allow natural reflow. For Mother, describe body adaptation with supportive art; for Baby, explain development with stage-appropriate anatomy. Use genuine distinct source-appropriate images when available. Fetal art must identify whether it is schematic, representative, measured or original textbook art; do not depict a precise week if source only supports a stage range. If no rights-cleared appropriate image exists use a clearly marked original conceptual illustration or simple text rather than guessing anatomy. Link to original reference when relevant. Keep opening concise so first weekly feature is discoverable.

### 3.4 Optional size comparison (Baby)
A compact warm-neutral card below hero and before current week content. A small fruit/vegetable illustration on left; “About the size of a [food]” and estimated length on right. Use a credible specific source and label crown–rump versus head-to-heel; convert consistently (1 inch = 2.54 cm), avoid excessive precision. Example Week 22: sweet potato, ~27.8 cm / 10.9 in; Week 23: large mango, ~28.9 cm / 11.4 in (NHS week pages). Comparison is approximate; produce varies, image not to scale, not an ultrasound estimate or clinical percentile. Do not invent weight or size; omit when unsupported, before conception, or inappropriate. No week-to-week growth graph derived from fruit images. If an actual growth reference graph is proposed, separately verify its population, units, centiles, measurement method, licensing and clinical interpretation.

## 4. Screen contract: the changing middle (main educational content)
Heading: “Your body around this week” or “What's developing around this week.” Use 2–4 high-value features per subject as editorial starting points, normally three in the reference. Fewer are better if evidence is limited. A feature is a compact illustrated row with left image (reference prototype about 115px wide, 4:3), right timing/context line when useful, plain bold title, short inviting description, chevron or equivalent click affordance. Thin neutral dividers; no repetitive heavy borders. Whole row opens its actual topic or reading. Keep the image clearly relevant: fetal hair, meconium, skeleton, lower airway branching, marrow, breast tissue, rib cage, foods, etc., not generic baby silhouettes for every item. Vary visuals with content, not for novelty alone.

### 4.1 Timing categories
Every feature must store separately:
- display stage/week; evidence window start/end or explicit broader phase; source ID or exact source locator; timing classification.
- developmental window: source directly describes a milestone in a range (e.g. weeks 23–25); do not invent half-week precision, compress a range to ±1 week, or equate a typical milestone with a required event.
- maternal experience: possible change in this stage (not universal or necessarily beginning this week).
- editorial spotlight: a relevant topic selected for learning now (e.g. nutrition, prevention, environment, exercise); explicitly not a new biological requirement, treatment start or dose schedule.
- future preparation: appropriately labeled reading ahead; no action deadline or premature intervention.
- safety: always accessible irrespective of feature placement, never deferred because not a feature this week.
If two consecutive weeks share a source window, vary the educational angle (structure → function → relationship → evidence) rather than fabricate new milestone onset. It is fine to retain a particularly relevant ongoing feature, but do not copy the whole section. If only a broad window is supported, say so. Do not manufacture daily or weekly supplement stacks.

### 4.2 Selection principles
Use existing research on maternal physiology, developmental biology, nutrition, exercise, skin, environmental exposure, preventive care, recovery, traditional/emerging practices, mental health, partner support and child health. Select what is relevant to actual stage, severity, evidence and user interest. Include overlooked questions and under-discussed mechanisms where supported; do not promote ineffective or unsafe interventions simply to make a page distinct. Prioritize strong evidence and practical affordable measures. A curious reader can access full sources. Aim for one clear scientific story per feature, not a sentence of generic caution followed by a link.

## 5. Screen contract: shared reading at the very bottom
After the changing middle, a thin divider, plain “Ongoing topics” heading and range subtitle. In reference pilot use **Weeks 20–27 · Shared mid-pregnancy reading**. This is an editorial grouping, not a biological start/end or an intervention period; much content also applies outside it. The canonical phase data retains PREG02 Weeks 14–27. Proposed later editorial grouping boundaries are flexible and must be documented, not presented as clinical deadlines. A compact two-column directory with small illustrative thumbnails or meaningful icons and plain labels; the earlier visual grid with rich images is acceptable for shared topics if not overlong, but priority is a compact lower layer. Each entry opens a topic grouping with relevant subsections. Do not require all categories to repeat in full every week. Do not remove access to shared articles merely because not featured. Urgent help remains globally accessible, independent of bottom navigation.

Mother stable reference categories:
- Skin: stretch marks (prevention, causes, creams, oils, collagen), comfort, acne/pigmentation, future treatments, hair and wound/scar recovery.
- Exercise/movement: activity/strength, pain, fetal movement, mood/sleep; stage-specific safety.
- Nutrition: food, supplement quality, DHA/fish, choline/iodine/folate, minerals, vitamin D, probiotics; cross-links to digestion and environment.
- Pelvic health/body: pelvic floor, abdomen, breast changes, birth preparation; separate pregnancy from postpartum.
- Digestion: nausea, vomiting, food safety, gut-health and traditional options.
- Circulation: leg symptoms, veins, compression research, blood/iron connection, urgent signs.
- Prenatal care: screening, appointments, oral health, preventive care, urgent signs.
- Environment: workplace/home, water/plastics/PFAS, air/heat/food exposures and affordable practical steps.
Baby stable reference categories:
- Brain: neural development and nutrient evidence; distinguish IQ claims and surrogate outcomes.
- Senses: hearing, touch, speech and sound research.
- Placenta: exchange, cord, fetal circulation, exposures.
- Movement: muscles/bones, patterns, maternal activity outcomes.
- Lungs: stage-appropriate respiratory development.
- Skin: fine hair and changing protective structures.
- Digestion: meconium, swallowing, nutrition connection.
- Blood/circulation: hematopoiesis and exchange.
- Growth/assessment: anatomy, growth research and screening; accessible as compact additional link or via suitable grouping. No inaccessible topic due to eight-item grid.
Shared groups should link to one canonical article body with phase/week context supplied by navigation, not duplicate copies of the research or unmarked archive jumps. The two pilot weeks share the existing 38 general + 12 skin + 2 developmental readings; rib comfort is an additional candidate. Other life stages require appropriate shared groups, not these exact eight categories.

## 6. Topic and subsection pages
Hierarchy: Journey → stage/week → Mother/Baby (or relevant role) → topic → specific question/article → evidence/original figure. Avoid unnecessary intermediate screens. Title, concise context breadcrumb, back link, compact meaningful opening visual, brief introduction. Topic groups list questions as clear rows with title, one-line description, optional small thumbnail and chevron. Skin can use plain groups Prevention / Comfort / Acne & pigmentation / Treatment after pregnancy / Hair / Healing & scar recovery. A group should not need another group merely to reach an answer. Deep links must resolve; back returns to exact previous context and scroll. Cross-link within prose to connected topics and stage-appropriate future reading. Avoid duplicating a headline as both H1 and H2. No hidden critical safety.

Each leaf is an illustrated small editorial article, not a database record or a generic two-sentence AI answer. Recommended order, adaptable to subject:
1. Short question-led or clear descriptive title; stage and topic context.
2. Opening image/figure appropriate to the actual question; caption/source/limitations.
3. Intro explaining the main point and why it matters.
4. Descriptive headings: biological mechanism or symptom context, practical relevance, evidence synthesis. Use two to four substantive sections as needed. A simple development reading need not pretend there is an intervention trial.
5. Meaningful comparisons/figures where warranted, with original data and interpretation. Avoid a numeric chart when no suitable verified numbers.
6. Material limitations/funding/sponsor role adjacent to the finding; normal disclosures in references.
7. Practical learning takeaway/what to discuss. Separate established care, evidence-informed choices, emerging research and unproven/harmful claims. No personal dosing or treatment clearance.
8. Normally two optional expansions: “More research & context” and “Studies & references.” Optional third “More about [topic]” only if useful. No nested details. Related rows may be visible instead of another expander. Avoid making the reader open five sections for one answer.
9. Visible stage-specific safety and review status, with urgent access. Safety overrides article reading; don't hide it under an expansion.
No forced number of words or reading grade. Concise at a glance, substantial when the question warrants. Preserve uncertainty, outcomes, timepoints and applicability. Use original sources as clickable footnotes near supported claims, stable numbering within an article (do not recycle [1] for a different source), reference list with title/year/link/source ID, and archived study details behind expansion. Cite biological descriptions separately from trial outcomes. Any source held, excluded or alias-held must fail closed; numeric blocks cannot be revived by editorial wording. Keep source record counts distinct from independent studies.

### 6.1 Gold-standard comparison examples
- Creams/moisturizers: trial cocoa butter 45.1% versus placebo lotion 48.8%, 175/210 completed, p=0.730, manufacturer supplied both lotions. Source-reported example (Osman et al. 2008); no clear prevention benefit, not proof of equivalence. Include absolute difference only with direction/denominator, never imply efficacy. Cochrane context, older search limits. Chart publication pending.
- Oil/massage: reported 20% combined vs 38.8% oil only vs 41.2% control; no massage-only arm, funding/safety unverified, no recommended bitter almond oil protocol. Clearly label study design and do not rank alternatives as safe.
- Collagen: adult skin aging meta-analysis reports differing subgroup significance by pharma funding and quality; adult hydration/elasticity/wrinkles not pregnancy prevention, no causal sponsor-effect inference, no commercial-free inference from undeclared funding. Qualitative matrix only if verified and reviewed.
- Choline: small feeding trial and seven-year follow-up same cohort, mixed evidence including separate null trial, public/commodity-industry/commercial support; separate nutrient role/adequacy from cognitive outcome. Do not transform experimental amounts into routine doses.
- Omega-3: ORIP 2.2% vs 2.0% birth before 34 weeks, adjusted RR and interval retained, no clear reduction in this trial; public/foundation support with donated capsules, separate from childhood cognition and other populations. No routine high-dose recommendation.
- Placenta: labeled anatomy or accurate conceptual exchange diagram distinguishing circulations; no mixing of blood implication or individual transfer rates.
These are examples of editorial direction and prior draft evidence, not authorized clinical claims or new chart approvals. Independently verify primary records and publication gate before exposing any findings.

## 7. Research integration contract
Use the existing scientific foundation first. Do not perform a broad new literature search for every week. Start with Stage 10 consolidated import, source index, canonical registry, claims, outcomes, nutrients, study arms, cohort relationships, publication gate, Stage 09 articles/timeline/actions/FAQ, source audit and topic dossiers. Reconcile latest independent appraisal/continuation and high-audit records before using a claim. Existing AI-authored conclusions are comparison material; fresh independent appraisal of actual papers is required. Do not equate a stored source ID with support for a new claim.
For each candidate article:
- Identify source window, parent phase, role, topic ID, canonical article ID, intended outcome and relevance. Preserve source IDs and citation locators.
- Review original methods, participants, comparators, follow-up, units/denominators, effect size/interval, harms, exclusions, missing data, registration, version, retraction/correction, directness and generalizability. Record evidence type and uncertainty. Avoid counting multiple reports of a cohort or overlapping reviews as independent.
- Record study-specific and review-specific funding, sponsor role, product donation, institutional ties, financial conflicts, disclosure status and exact locator. Unknown is unknown. A public grant plus commercial donation is mixed support; manufacturer participation is not automatically fraud; independence is not proven by no disclosure. Evaluate bias with methods, not funding alone.
- Compare conflicting studies and established versus exploratory approaches on equal standards. Distinguish association from causation, biological plausibility from clinical benefit, surrogate from meaningful outcomes, non-significance from equivalence, animal/adult evidence from maternal/fetal/child applicability.
- Include practical, low-cost precautions and clinician questions where appropriate; avoid overmedicalization, fear, coercion, detox claims and unverified product recommendations. Keep clear about what is uncertain and what would change confidence.
- Use only source-backed developmental windows and regional guidance. US clinical context remains distinct from NHS developmental education; no importing UK visit schedules as US requirements.
- Keep nutrition targets versus supplements separate, stage/age/sex/life-stage specific, intake versus dose, food sources, dietary patterns, existing medications, contraindications, tolerable upper limits and adverse effects. Only validated data may become a chart; no clinical dose records or automatic schedules while locked.
- Record any genuinely missing evidence as backlog with a focused question. Do not silently fill a gap from memory. Material safety and clinical review cannot be replaced by AI agreement.

## 8. Scientific visual standard
Purpose: art explains biology, mechanisms, meaningful comparisons or practical context. A decorative photo can support a food/experience story but cannot establish anatomy or efficacy. Prioritize existing appropriate OpenStax/Servier/public agency/peer-reviewed source figures; retain original source links where embedding is not cleared. Verify exact asset file/version, creator, license, derivative rights, restrictions, attribution, original bytes/hash, alt text, anatomical accuracy, developmental stage, reviewer and clinical release status. Respect source license even if a screenshot appears online. Don't hotlink unapproved third-party images, introduce trackers or infer license from brand. Use local approved assets only when gate permits, otherwise original source link or original conceptual art explicitly labeled.
Visuals in chat reference are illustrative web lookups, not approved assets. Preserve the desired subject, composition and feel; they are not permissions or deployed screenshots.
- Hero: one appropriate stage illustration, compact; do not claim exact week anatomy without verification.
- Weekly features: distinct meaningful subjects (hair, intestines, skeleton vs lung branching, bone marrow, fetal movement; maternal skin/pelvis/food vs breast/ribs/iron food). Crop with care; labels readable, maintain aspect ratio.
- Shared tiles: stable recognizability; compact thumbnails/icons, no need for new art each week if content identical.
- Article diagrams: caption differentiates conceptual, textbook, original study graph and measured data. Avoid same generic repeated image across unrelated leaves. Choose focused figure for specific question; no fabricated anatomical details. Provide text alternative and original source attribution.
- Quantitative charts: state outcome/timepoint/unit/population/comparator, sample/denominator, interval level and source; sensible axes (zero for count/proportion bars, null=1 for ratios/null=0 for differences), no misleading truncation or colors. Preserve review holds. Compare like with like; never make a funding-effect chart from incomparable significant/non-significant subgroups. If no numeric approval use honest qualitative explanation.
- Video/animation: optional only if it genuinely explains motion/biology and rights/safety allow; no autoplay or simulated clinical procedure instructions. Keep accessible captions and static alternative.
- Performance: bundle permitted assets efficiently, prevent layout shift, ensure offline behavior, never make external requests without user intent for unapproved media. Test actual mobile images, text contrast, safe areas, dark/alternate themes and 200% zoom.

## 10. Adaptation across the entire journey
The hierarchy stays the same; units and subject labels change with biology and family needs.
- Preconception (PRE01–PRE06): optional relative months, two parents or shared preparation rather than implying pregnancy. Opening: purpose of phase; middle: current preparation focus; bottom: shared long-range planning / nutrition / fertility / exposures / activity / mental health / preventive care. Include paternal and maternal research, folate timing, fertility evidence and limits, medication/vaccine questions. No fertility guarantees, forced timing or conception countdown. Before pregnancy or weeks 1–2, no fetus size/hero; explain gestational dating.
- Pregnancy (PREG01–03): week chapters as above; stage overview uses same pattern with links to weeks and shared reading. Early gestation must distinguish embryonic/fetal terminology and dating. Late pregnancy includes birth/feeding preparation and newborn transition without self-induction, home maneuver or guaranteed outcomes. Birth timing varies; allow beyond week 40 navigation or clinical advice boundary without inventing milestone data.
- Birth (BIRTH01): shared event chapter, Mother and Newborn or clearly paired tracks; opening context, current transition and care-team questions, then general labor/feeding/skin-to-skin/newborn safety. Clinical team and individual circumstances govern. No mandatory sequence for every birth or induction instructions.
- Postpartum (M01–M07): Mother recovery timeline by actual days/weeks/months; parallel baby track, feeding choice and family support. Opening phase context, middle healing/follow-up/comfort/mental health/function topics, shared recovery library. Recovery ranges are not deadlines/automatic exercise clearance. Distinguish cesarean/perineal injury, wound closure, pelvic symptoms and elective cosmetic care. Keep maternal urgent signs accessible through first year per reviewed guidance; stage-specific escalation.
- Newborn/infant (C01–C07): age-window chapter, newborn/child focus and caregiver context. Stage-specific feeding cues, sleep safety, jaundice/screening, illness, responsive interaction, motor/play, complementary food and allergens/readiness; shared feeding/safety/preventive-care library. Use chronological versus corrected age appropriately, no infant feeding or sleep-training automatic prescriptions. Milestone windows are not performance scores. Urgent illness guidance immediately accessible. No diagnosis, forced solids or choking-risk foods.
- Toddler/early child (C08–C11): age-appropriate language, play, movement, nutrition, dental, safety and preventive visits. Readiness not a toilet-learning deadline, no intelligence ranking. Ages 2–5 secondary to core pregnancy, postpartum and birth–2 years, but still in tracker. Allow stage browsing without collecting identity/health metrics. Use age-appropriate and rights-cleared visual assets.
For every phase use the same principle: opening orientation, current focused learning, general shared reading below; not repeated arbitrary weekly tasks. Long phases can have meaningful subchapters where data supports them.

No public deployment, no clinical advice automation, no auto-selected treatment or personal diagnosis.

## 12. Definition of completion
A chapter is **research mapped** when source/claim/article applicability and holds are recorded; **editorial draft** when subject-specific opening/current features/shared group and linked articles exist; **implemented candidate** when functioning routes/components and visual assets or permitted references are integrated; **software verified** only after exact commit tests/build; **visually verified** only after real mobile screenshots and interaction testing; **owner accepted** only after explicit owner review of actual app; **clinically cleared** only after independent qualified review and authorized publication gates. Distinct statuses; none imply the next. Do not check off what was merely proposed or written. Count source records separately from studies, count canonical articles separately from routes. Track each stage individually. Owner approval of chat direction does not establish screenshot acceptance.
